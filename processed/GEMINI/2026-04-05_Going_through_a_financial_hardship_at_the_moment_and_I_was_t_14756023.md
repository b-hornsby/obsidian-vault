### GEMINI
This $20k goal is a classic "gold rush" strategy: high ticket, low volume. It sounds great on paper because the math is simple, but your hesitation about "larping" is your intuition flagging a massive execution gap. If you feel like a fraud, you’ll sell like a fraud, and you’ll fail. Here is the unfiltered reality of your situation and the plan to bridge the gap between "vibe coding" and a $4,000 service. The Reality Check The "Larp" Risk: You aren't "larping" if you deliver $4,000 of value. You are larping if you sell a "vision" you can't technically support when the first bug hits. High-paying clients don't pay for "vibe coding"; they pay for reliability and ROI. The Opportunity Cost: Spending weeks trying to land one $4k client while in a financial hardship is high-risk. If you miss, you have $0 and wasted time. The Scaling Trap: Your cousin’s math ($1,500/mo scaling to $2,500) assumes 100% retention. In the agency world, churn is real. You need a product that is "sticky." The Strategy: From "Vibe" to "Value" To stop feeling like a larp, you need to transition from "someone who plays with AI" to "an Automation Architect." 1. Productize the "Vibe" Don't sell "coding" or "websites." Sell Time Recovery. The Offer: "I build autonomous workflows that handle [Specific Task] so you don't have to hire a VA." The Proof: Show a working agent you’ve built for yourself. If you can't show it working, don't sell it yet. 2. The "Bridge" Pricing Model If $4,000 feels like a lie, don't start there. Phase A (The Beta): Sell a "Proof of Concept" for $1,000. It’s low enough to close quickly but high enough to cover bills. Phase B (The Retainer): Once the tool works, charge $500/mo for "Maintenance and Optimization." This creates the $6k/mo floor your cousin is talking about without the "scammy" feeling. 3. Strategic Action Plan Audit Your Stack: You’re using agentic tools and local LLMs. Pick one specific business use case (e.g., automated lead scraping to CRM, or an AI-driven IT helpdesk for small shops). The "Fuck it we ball" Outreach: Reach out to 10 local businesses or crypto projects. Don't ask for $4k. Offer a "Performance Audit" where you show them exactly where an AI agent could replace a manual process. Documentation is Credibility: The difference between a hobbyist and a pro is documentation. Give your clients a "System Architecture" map. It makes the "vibe code" look like an engineered solution. The Bottom Line Confidence comes from competence. You are currently underestimating the gap between "building for yourself" and "building for a paying client." Stop worrying about the $20k total and focus on landing one client at a price point that makes you feel like an expert, not a pretender. What is the single most functional, "battle-ready" automation you have built to date? That is your $4,000 product. Everything else is noise.

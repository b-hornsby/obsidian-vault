### GEMINI
The terminal output confirms that VRR is already set to "Never" on both of your monitors. Since you’ve already handled the nano flags and the Window Rules, the technical "infrastructure" is now solid. If it is still flickering and laggy, we are down to the final two variables: The Compositor Pipeline and PipeWire. 1. The "NVIDIA-Wayland" Sync Fix On NVIDIA + Wayland, the most common remaining cause of flickering is a "race condition" in the frame renderer. Open your terminal. Run this command to check if your NVIDIA driver is using the modern synchronization method: cat /sys/module/nvidia_drm/parameters/modeset The Result: If it says Y, you're good. If it says N, that’s your flickering culprit. (On CachyOS, this is usually Y by default, but it's worth checking). 2. The Final Vesktop Internal Check Since you couldn't find "Capturer Settings," we need to ensure Vesktop isn't trying to use an old X11 capture method by mistake. Open Vesktop. Go to Settings (Gear icon) -> Vencord (on the left) -> Plugins. Search for "Fake Screen Share" or "Platform Spoofer." The Fix: If either of these are ON, turn them OFF. They can cause your 3070 to output corrupted frames to the stream. 3. The "Hardware vs. Software" Encode Test This is the most "brutally honest" part of the fix. Even with an RTX 3070, NVIDIA's Linux encoder (NVENC) can be unstable on Wayland. Go to Settings -> Voice & Video. Scroll down to Video Codec. Turn OFF "OpenH264 Video Codec" and "Hardware Acceleration." Why: This forces your i7-12700K to handle the stream. With 12 cores, your CPU will barely feel the load, and "Software Encoding" is almost always 100% flicker-free compared to GPU encoding on Linux. The Verdict You’ve done the heavy lifting. You have: Confirmed VRR is disabled (via kscreen-doctor). Forced Native Wayland (via nano). Optimized Window Rules (Accept Focus/Focus Stealing). Test the stream now. If the flickering is gone but it's still "laggy," go back to Step 3 and turn off Hardware Acceleration inside Discord's Voice & Video settings. That is the final "logistics" bottleneck that usually clears everything up.

Question: A graphic artist from a company is using a LCD display monitor that is showing several black pixels. They turn the power off and restart the display, but the same pixels remain black so they ask the company's technician for help to resolve the problem. The technician needs to resolve the problem quickly, keeping the cost incurred to a minimum. What should they do? Options: Replace the display. Change the display resolution. Replace the display cable. Replace the video adapter. Change the display refresh frequency. Analysis of the problem and options: Problem: "Several black pixels" that remain black even after a power cycle. This strongly indicates dead pixels. Dead pixels are physical defects on the LCD panel where the pixel no longer receives power or can change color, resulting in a permanent black spot. Option Analysis: Change the display resolution / Change the display refresh frequency: These are software settings and would not fix a physical defect like a dead pixel. Replace the display cable: A faulty cable might cause flickering, color distortion, or no signal at all, but it's highly unlikely to cause specific pixels to be permanently black while the rest of the display functions. If it were a cable issue, the problem would likely affect a larger area or the entire screen, or be intermittent. Replace the video adapter (graphics card): Similar to the cable, a faulty video adapter usually causes widespread display issues (e.g., artifacts, no signal, incorrect colors across the entire screen) rather than isolated dead pixels. The problem description points to the display itself. Replace the display: This directly addresses the hardware component that has dead pixels. Since the pixels remain black after a power cycle, they are physically damaged and cannot be fixed by software adjustments or replacing other components. Given the criteria to "resolve the problem quickly, keeping the cost incurred to a minimum," replacing the display is the most direct and often the only effective solution for dead pixels. While a new display has a cost, attempting to fix individual pixels is usually not feasible or cost-effective for a business technician. Conclusion: The presence of "several black pixels" that persist after a power cycle points directly to a hardware defect in the display panel itself (dead pixels). The only effective solution in this scenario is to replace the display. The user has correctly selected this option.

In your third image, you have already found the primary power toggle: USB power delivery in Soft Off state (S5) is set to Enabled. This is good; it means your motherboard is physically capable of keeping the Yeti powered when the system is "off". However, your second image shows that Enhanced C-states and CPU C-states are still active (Enabled/Auto). If you haven't switched those to Disabled yet, do it now. The Final BIOS Checklist Based on your specific ASUS screens, perform these exact moves before exiting: In "Onboard Devices Configuration" (Image 3): USB power delivery in Soft Off state (S5): Leave this Enabled. In "CPU - Power Management Control" (Image 2): Enhanced C-states: Change to Disabled. CPU C-states: Change to Disabled. Navigate to "APM Configuration" (Not pictured, but in the Advanced tab): ErP Ready: Ensure this is Disabled. Moving to the OS Level (CachyOS) Once you hit F10 and boot back into CachyOS, we need to address the "Software Handshake." If the Yeti still doesn't appear, it’s because the Linux kernel is trying to save power on that specific USB port. Do this as soon as you reach your desktop: Open your terminal. Run lsusb. Look for a line that says Blue Microphones or Yeti Stereo Microphone. Note the ID numbers (they look like 046d:0ab7). Why this matters: Even with the BIOS "forcing" power, CachyOS is a performance-tuned distro. Its power management scripts will try to suspend "idle" USB devices. We need to create a udev rule to tell the kernel: "This specific ID is never allowed to sleep." Are you back at your desktop? Paste the output of lsusb and I'll give you the exact string to copy-paste into your terminal to finish this.

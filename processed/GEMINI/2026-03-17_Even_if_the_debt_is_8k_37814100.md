# Even if the debt is 8k?

### USER
Even if the debt is 8k?

### GEMINI
Yes, even if the debt is $8,000. In fact, the higher the amount, the more important it is that you follow the steps I gave you. Here is the "brutally honest" breakdown of why the amount doesn't change the strategy—it only changes the urgency. 1. The "Zero Dollars" Reality Whether you owe $800 or $8,000, if you don't have the money, you don't have the money. At $11 an hour, you are what the legal world calls "Judgment Proof." The Protection: Ohio law protects low-income earners. Because your wages are so close to the protected threshold ($217.50/week after taxes), a debt collector legally cannot garnish your paycheck in most cases. The Math: A court won't order you to pay $8,000 if your income only covers your basic survival (rent, food, transit). 2. Why $8k Makes the "Answer" Critical If the debt was $500, they might not bother with a long fight. For $8,000, they are much more likely to play the long game. If you don't file an Answer, they get a "Default Judgment." This stays on your record for years. The moment you get a better-paying IT job, they will see that on your credit report and immediately start garnishing 25% of your new, higher salary. If you do file an Answer, you freeze them. You force them to wait for a trial. This buys you the time you need to find that better job and settle on your terms later, rather than having them take it by force. 3. The "Fee Waiver" is Your Proof Filing the Civil Fee Waiver (Form 20) alongside your Answer is a strategic move. It’s a sworn document that tells the Judge and the law firm's attorney: "Look at my income. Even if you win this case, you aren't getting paid today." When a law firm sees an $8,000 case against someone who is legally indigent, they often stop spending money on expensive legal fees to fight you. They might just let the case sit or offer you a tiny settlement just to close the file. 4. Community Legal Aid is a Must Because the debt is $8,000, you are now in "High Stakes" territory. Call them: (330) 535-4191. Since you are low-income, they will likely give you a lawyer for free. That lawyer can look for technicalities—like whether the debt collector actually has the original contract—to get the $8,000 case dismissed entirely. It's just a number on a page until a Judge signs a judgment—and your goal is to make sure that doesn't happen.

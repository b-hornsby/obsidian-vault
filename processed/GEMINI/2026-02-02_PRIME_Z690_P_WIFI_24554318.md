### GEMINI
On the ASUS PRIME Z690-P WIFI, your USB ports are not all created equal. Based on the technical manual and your lsusb output, here is the brutal truth: You are currently plugging the Yeti into a port that is "fake" 3.0. The "Blue" Port Trap On your specific board, the two Blue ports are technically USB 3.2 Gen 1 (5Gbps). However, ASUS often routes these through a shared hub on the chipset that also handles your legacy peripherals. This is why lsusb -t shows your "Blue" port landing on Bus 001 (the slow 480M bus). Your "Golden" Port There is only one USB-A port on the back of your motherboard that is a True Gen 2 (10Gbps) port. It is usually Teal (Light Blue/Green) or Red. Location: It is typically the single port located directly above or next to the USB-C port. The Goal: If you plug the Yeti into that Teal/Red port, it should move to Bus 002. The Precision Plan (Musical Chairs) Since you don't have an adapter for the USB-C port, you have to be strategic with the high-speed lanes you do have. Evict the Mouse: Take the Razer mouse dongle out of the USB-C port (or wherever it is) and plug it into one of the topmost Black ports (USB 2.0). Why? A mouse dongle is a low-bandwidth device. Putting it on your fastest port is like driving a lawnmower on a Formula 1 track. Move the Yeti to the "Teal" Port: Find the USB-A port that is a different color from the dark blue ones. Plug the Yeti there. Run lsusb -t one last time. If the Yeti is under Bus 002, you have officially "escaped" the traffic jam. Why this is the end of the road By putting the Yeti on Bus 002 and having autosuspend=-1 active, you have removed every single software and hardware excuse for it to fail. Software: The kernel is now forced to keep power on (-1). Hardware: The device is now on its own dedicated 10Gbps/20Gbps controller lane, far away from the "noise" of your keyboard and mouse. Try that Teal/Red port now. Does lsusb -t finally show something—anything—under Bus 002?

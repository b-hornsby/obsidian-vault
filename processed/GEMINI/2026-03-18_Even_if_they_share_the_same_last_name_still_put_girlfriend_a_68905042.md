### GEMINI
Yes, even if you share the same last name, you should still list her as a girlfriend or roommate. Since you aren't legally married, you are not a single legal household in the eyes of the Ohio Supreme Court. Under Ohio Revised Code 2323.311, the fee waiver is based on your financial situation. If you aren't married and don't file taxes jointly, her income is hers and your income is yours. How to handle this on the Form 20: Relationship Section: Write "Girlfriend" or "Roommate." Do not check any box that says "Spouse." Income Section: Look for the column that asks for "Spouse (If Living in Household)." Leave that column completely blank or write "N/A" or "0". Total Monthly Income: This should only reflect your $11/hour earnings. Why this is a tactical advantage: If you were to include her income (and her dad's), your "Household Income" might look too high to qualify for the free waiver. By keeping it strictly to yourself, you stay well under the 187.5% Federal Poverty Limit (which for a household of 1 in Ohio is currently about $1,993/month). At $11/hour, you are right in that "sweet spot" to get the fees waived. One final "Brutally Honest" tip: The Clerk or the Judge doesn't care about your dating life; they only care if you have the legal right to access someone else's money to pay for this lawsuit. Since you aren't married, you don't. Pro-Tip for the Clerk's window: If the Clerk sees the same last name and asks if you're married, just say: "No, we aren't married, and we keep our finances completely separate." That usually ends the conversation. You're all set for tomorrow. Do you have your State ID and your three copies of the Answer ready to go into your bag right now?

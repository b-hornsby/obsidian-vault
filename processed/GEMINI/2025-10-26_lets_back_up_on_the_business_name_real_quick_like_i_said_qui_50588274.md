### GEMINI
That's a smart move to pause and nail the name. You're right, "Quick Fix Solutions" is a little generic and does lean toward a service like an auto body shop or even plumbing. Your cousin has a good point, though—the word "quick" and the idea of a recognizable, easily repeatable phrase are very strong branding elements. The goal is to capture that feeling of speed and reliability without sounding like a national chain. Here are a few naming approaches that aim to hit that sweet spot: recognizable, great sound, focused on your kind of 'fix' (which I'll assume is tech, consulting, or general business help), and not overly commercial. 1. The "Quick" Alternatives (Focus on Speed & Efficiency) These names keep the desirable "quick" feeling but use more sophisticated words than "fix" or "solution," making them less likely to be mistaken for a physical trade. Swift Ascent: Implies a fast rise or improvement for a client's business. It sounds professional and forward-thinking. Rapid Catalyst: A catalyst is something that speeds up a reaction without being consumed itself. This suggests you are the element that quickly makes their business better. Velocity Consult (or Velocity Advisors): Velocity is a very professional, business-focused word for speed. It's direct and authoritative. The Express Method: Simple, clear, and gives a sense of a premium, fast-tracked service. 2. The Recognizable "Sound" (Focus on Catchy Alliteration or Rhythm) These names have a pleasant flow and are easy to remember, which is great for word-of-mouth. Elevate Logic: A nice, smooth rhythm that suggests you use smart thinking to lift their business. The Clarity Core: Short, strong, and emphasizes getting to the heart of the problem quickly. Impact Insight: Alliterative and professional, suggesting you provide fast-acting, high-value advice. 3. The "Fix" without Sounding like a Plumber (Focus on Resolution) These use words that evoke problem-solving but in a strategic, business-focused context. Pivot Point: A strong, directional name that suggests you quickly identify the change-up needed to succeed. Resolve Group: Simple, action-oriented, and clearly states your purpose is to resolve issues. The Blueprint Agency: Suggests you deliver the essential, perfect plan quickly. My Top Recommendations for Your Criteria: Swift Ascent: (Professional, quick-sounding, not too commercial) Velocity Consult: (Authoritative, clear, and business-focused) Elevate Logic: (Catchy, good sound, easy to remember) Do any of these strike a chord, or does a specific word in one of them (like Swift, Velocity, or Impact) inspire a new idea?

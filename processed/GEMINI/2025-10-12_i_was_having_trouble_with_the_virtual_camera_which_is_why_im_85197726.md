### GEMINI
That is completely understandable. Troubleshooting virtual cameras can be frustrating, and it's always best to work directly within the tool you are comfortable with. The challenge is that TikTok LIVE Studio does not currently have the built-in compositing features needed to perform the "duplicate, scale, and blur" effect you're describing. In most streaming and video editing software (like OBS, Premiere, DaVinci Resolve), the effect relies on these three key capabilities: Duplicating a Live Source: Creating a second, independent layer of the same live game feed. Layering: Placing the two sources on top of each other (the clear one on top, the blurred one on the bottom). Applying a Filter: Having a dedicated Blur filter to apply only to the background layer. Based on the available information and community feedback, TikTok LIVE Studio lacks the ability to do steps 1 and 3 simultaneously on a single Game Capture source. Your Options for Achieving the Effect within TikTok Studio Since you want to stream only with TikTok Studio, you must rely on its existing features. There are two potential workarounds, though they are less clean than the OBS method: Workaround 1: Using a Static Image Background (Easiest) If the game's display size in your layout is small (like a centered square/rectangle), you can use a blurred Image as the background instead of a duplicated live feed. Capture a Still Image: Take a high-resolution screenshot of your current gameplay. Blur the Image: Use a simple photo editor (like a free online one, or Paint.net/GIMP) to aggressively blur that screenshot. Save the blurred image. Add to TikTok Studio: Click Add Source $\rightarrow$ Image. Import your blurred screenshot. Scale it to fill the entire 9:16 background of your stream. Place it at the bottom of your source list (the very back layer). Add your primary Game Capture source on top of the blurred image. Resize and position the game capture so that the entire clear gameplay area is visible in the center, bordered by your blurred background image. Pro: Simple and won't stress your CPU much. Con: The background is static and won't move with the live game. Workaround 2: Using the Webcam Background Feature on the Game (Complex & Unreliable) TikTok Studio has a Video Background feature, primarily intended for webcams, but you might be able to trick it into applying a blur effect. Add Game Capture: Add your game as a primary source (Game Capture or Window Capture). Apply Effect (if possible): In your source list, click on the three dots next to your Game Capture source, or look for an Effects or Filter tab in its properties. If there is a "Background Removal" or "Video Background" option, select it and see if it has a Blur option. The problem is: This feature is primarily designed to detect a person in the foreground (like a webcam feed) and blur the rest. It's unlikely to correctly detect the main game display and blur the black bars surrounding it. It might just blur the whole thing or do nothing. If your ultimate goal is a professional, clean, dynamic blurred-background effect, you will need to revisit the OBS Virtual Camera setup. If you describe the issue you were having with it (e.g., black screen, high CPU usage, etc.), I can help you troubleshoot it, as that remains the industry-standard method for this specific effect.

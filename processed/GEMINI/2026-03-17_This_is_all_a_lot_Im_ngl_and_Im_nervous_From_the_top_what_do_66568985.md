### GEMINI
I hear you. It’s a heavy realization when a legal document shows up at your door, and it’s completely normal to feel like the walls are closing in. But let’s get one thing straight: This is a math and paperwork problem, not a character flaw. You have a job in logistics; think of this as a "discrepancy report" that you need to resolve before it messes up the warehouse flow. Here is your exact, step-by-step checklist to take control of this today. Step 1: The "Stay in the Game" Response (28-Day Deadline) Even if you owe every penny, you must file a written "Answer" with the Stow Municipal Court. If you don't, you lose by default, and they can start taking money from your paycheck (garnishment). Get the Form: Go to the Stow Municipal Court Civil Forms website and download the "Defendant’s Answer" or go to the court at 4400 Courthouse Drive, Stow, OH and ask for one. Fill it Out: Put the Case Number at the top. For the paragraphs where they claim you owe money, check the box that says "Deny" or "Deny for lack of knowledge." * Why? Because "Deny" in a courtroom doesn't mean "I'm a liar." It means "I am forcing you to prove your math and your legal right to collect this." File it: Take the original to the courthouse. It is free to file an Answer in Stow. Bring two extra copies and ask the clerk to "Time Stamp" them. Step 2: The "Paper Trail" Service You have to let the other side's lawyer know you've responded. Take one of those time-stamped copies and mail it to the lawyer’s address listed on your summons (Zwicker & Associates). Send it via Certified Mail at the post office. It costs about $4.00, but that receipt is your "Get Out of Jail Free" card if they try to say you didn't respond. Step 3: Call for Free Local Reinforcements Since you’re in Akron, you don’t have to do this alone. Call Community Legal Aid: (330) 535-4191. Tell them: "I just got served a summons from Stow Municipal Court and I need help responding." * They are experts at this. They can often spot technicalities (like the debt being too old) that could get the whole thing thrown out. Step 4: Negotiate (Optional but Smart) Once your "Answer" is filed, you have leverage. The law firm now knows you aren't an easy target. You can call the law firm and say: "I can't pay the full amount, but I can offer [Amount] as a lump sum or [Amount] per month." Crucial: If you reach an agreement, do not pay a cent until they send you a signed "Settlement Agreement" in writing. The Opportunity Cost of Doing Nothing If you ignore this, the court will eventually grant a Default Judgment. In Ohio, they can take up to 25% of your take-home pay every single pay period until the debt (plus interest and court costs) is paid. Filing that Answer stops that process in its tracks.

# What do you mean sweet/funky?

### USER
What do you mean sweet/funky?

### GEMINI
The terms "sweet/funky" are used to describe the particular unpleasant odors that arise from bacterial or yeast contamination—basically, the start of fermentation or rot—in organic matter like mushrooms. In the context of dried mushrooms, this smell is a serious warning sign of spoilage. Here is what that kind of smell typically indicates: 1. "Sweet" as in Fermentation/Rot This is not a pleasant, sugary-sweet smell. It's often associated with compounds produced when bacteria or yeast begin to break down the sugars, proteins, and carbohydrates in the mushroom due to excess moisture. What it smells like: It can resemble old, cloying fruit, sickly-sweet bread, or a slightly alcoholic/boozy odor. Think of something that is starting to ferment or rot. What it means: The mushroom was not dried completely, or it was exposed to moisture during storage, allowing microbial growth. This is a sign of bacterial or yeast contamination. 2. "Funky" as in Putrid/Off "Funky" is a general term for a strong, pungent, and distinctly "off" odor. In the case of spoiled food, including mushrooms, it often refers to compounds released during decomposition. What it smells like: This can be described as sour, rancid, faintly like stinky cheese, old socks, or sometimes even a foul, sharp, vinegar-like, or vaguely fishy/ammonia smell. What it means: This smell is a strong indicator of decomposition and the presence of harmful bacteria or potentially mold. In Summary: A properly dried mushroom should have a mild, earthy, or almost neutral smell. If you open a container of dried mushrooms and are hit with a distinct, strong odor that is sweet/sickly, sour, or pungent/funky, the general safety advice is to discard them as they are likely contaminated and unsafe to eat.

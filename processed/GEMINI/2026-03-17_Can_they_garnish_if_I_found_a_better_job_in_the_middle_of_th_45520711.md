# Can they garnish if I found a better job in the middle of this?

### USER
Can they garnish if I found a better job in the middle of this?

### GEMINI
Yes, they can, but not immediately. This is why filing your "Answer" is so important. If you find a new job making more than $11/hour while this lawsuit is active, here is the technical breakdown of what happens: 1. Garnishment Requires a Judgment First In Ohio, a debt collector cannot just call your new boss and take money. They must first win the lawsuit and get a "Final Judgment" from the judge. If you do nothing: They get a "Default Judgment" in about 30 days. They can then use that to find your new employer and start taking 25%. If you file your Answer: You hit the "Pause" button. The case moves to a trial or mediation schedule. This usually takes 3 to 9 months. During this time, they cannot garnish a single cent from your new job, no matter how much you make. 2. The "Moving Target" Advantage Debt collectors find your job through credit reports or employment verification databases (like The Work Number). If you switch jobs, it takes time for their systems to update. By filing your Answer, you buy yourself months of "safe" income at your new higher rate. You can use that extra money to build an emergency fund or offer a lump-sum settlement to make the $8k debt go away for good. 3. Ohio’s Protection still applies Even at a better job, they can never take your whole check. Ohio law restricts them to the lesser of: 25% of your take-home pay. The amount by which your weekly pay exceeds $217.50. 4. The Opportunity Cost If you don't file the Answer because you're busy with a new job, you are handing them a "blank check" to garnish you later. Imagine this: You land a great IT job making $25/hr. If they have a Default Judgment from today, they will see that new income and immediately take $250+ out of every weekly check. The alternative: You file the Answer now. You get the $25/hr job. You save up $2,000. You call them and say, "I'll give you $2k today to close this $8k file forever." Because you fought the case, they are much more likely to say yes. The "Bottom Line" Plan File the Answer and Fee Waiver this week. This protects you for months, regardless of where you work. Focus on the job hunt. Don't mention the lawsuit to new employers; they don't need to know unless a garnishment order actually arrives (which won't happen if you're fighting the case). If you get a job: Use the first few paychecks to get stable, then contact Community Legal Aid to help you settle the debt for a fraction of the $8k. The $11/hr you make now qualifies you for the free Fee Waiver and free Legal Aid. Use those perks today to protect the higher income you'll have tomorrow.

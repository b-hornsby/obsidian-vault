This is your step-by-step combat plan. In the Stow Municipal Court, "doing nothing" is an automatic loss. The good news: Stow is one of the few courts that does not charge a fee to file an Answer. It costs you $0 to stay in the game, but it costs you everything to ignore it. Step 1: Draft Your "Answer" (Right Now) You don't need a lawyer to file a basic response. In Stow, you can simply write this on a standard piece of paper (8.5" x 11"). You must include: The Case Caption: The Court Name (Stow Municipal Court), Plaintiff’s name (the bank/collector), Defendant’s name (You), and the Case Number. The Responses: For every numbered paragraph in their Complaint, write: "Defendant [admits/denies/denies for lack of knowledge] the allegations in paragraph [Number]." Rule of Thumb: If you aren't 100% sure the math is right, Deny it. This forces them to prove it with actual statements and contracts. The Certificate of Service: At the very bottom, you must include this exact sentence: "I certify that a copy of this Answer was sent by ordinary U.S. Mail on [Date] to Counsel for Plaintiff, [Attorney Name and Address from the Summons]." Step 2: The Physical Filing (Before the 28th Day) Don't mail it to the court; walk it in. Where: 4400 Courthouse Drive, Stow, Ohio 44224. When: Monday – Friday, 8:00 AM – 4:30 PM. The "Timestamp": Bring three copies. Give the original to the Clerk. Ask them to "Timestamp" your other two copies. One is for your records; the other is for Step 3. Step 3: Serve the Plaintiff Mail one of those timestamped copies to the attorney listed on the papers (Zwicker & Associates). Use Certified Mail at the post office so you have a receipt. This is your "shield" if they try to claim you never responded. Step 4: Call for Reinforcements (Optional but Recommended) Since you're in Akron, you have access to Community Legal Aid. They specifically help with debt defense. Phone: (330) 535-4191 Address: 50 South Main Street, Suite 800, Akron. Why: They can tell you if the debt is "time-barred" (too old to sue over) or if the collector even has the legal right to sue you. Critical Warnings The 28-Day Rule: This is 28 calendar days, not business days. If your 28th day falls on a weekend, you must file by the Friday before. Don't ignore it because "it's true": Even if you owe the money, filing an Answer gives you leverage to negotiate a settlement (often for 40-60% of the total) because it proves you aren't a "easy win."

# So this is manageable is what you’re saying?

### USER
So this is manageable is what you’re saying?

### GEMINI
It is 100% manageable, but you have to stay disciplined. Dealing with two different debt collectors for the same original creditor (Discover) is a classic "divide and conquer" tactic used by debt buyers. They hope you'll get overwhelmed and ignore one, allowing them to get an easy win. Here is the perspective you need: You aren't just "paying bills" anymore—you are managing a legal defense. Why this is manageable: The Lawsuit ($8,000): You already have the Answer and Fee Waiver ready. Filing these on Monday effectively "mutes" this fire. Once filed, the bank's lawyers have to actually do work (which costs them money) to prove they own the debt. Most of the time, they'd rather settle for pennies or wait months than fight a "judgment proof" defendant. The New Collection Notice: This is just a piece of mail right now, not a court order. You have the upper hand here because the law (FDCPA) says that if you dispute it in writing within 30 days, they must stop all collection efforts until they send you proof. Your Financial Status: Your current income ($11/hr at 28 hours) is actually your strongest armor. Even if they "won," there is very little they could legally take from you in Ohio. You are what lawyers call "Judgment Proof." Monday's "Two-Pronged" Execution Plan: Since you're heading to the courthouse anyway, handle both issues in one trip. Part 1: The Courthouse (8:00 AM – 4:30 PM) Go to: Stow Municipal Court, 1st Floor, Civil Division. Action: File your Answer (3 copies) and your Form 20 (1 copy). The Result: The $8,000 lawsuit is now a "contested case." You've won the first round. Part 2: The Post Office (Right after the Court) Go to: The nearest Post Office (there is one at 3000 Graham Rd, Stow). Mail #1: Send the lawyer's copy of your Answer (the one the Clerk stamped) via regular mail. Mail #2: Send a Debt Validation Letter for the new collection notice via Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested. Draft: Debt Validation Letter (For the NEW notice) Fill this out, print it, and mail it Monday. To: [Name of the New Collection Agency from your notice] Address: [Their Address] Re: Account # [Account Number on the Notice] To Whom It May Concern, I am writing to formally dispute the validity of the debt referenced in your notice dated [Date on the notice]. Under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA), 15 U.S.C. § 1692g, I am requesting that you provide verification of this debt. Please provide the following: The name and address of the original creditor. A complete breakdown of the alleged amount owed, including interest and fees. Evidence that your company has the legal right to collect this specific debt (Chain of Title). Pursuant to the FDCPA, all collection activity must cease until this validation is provided. Sincerely, [Your Name] [Your Address] Does that take the weight off your shoulders for the weekend? You have a plan for the lawsuit and a shield for the new collector. Would you like me to find the exact name and address of that new collection agency for you so you can just copy-paste it into the letter?

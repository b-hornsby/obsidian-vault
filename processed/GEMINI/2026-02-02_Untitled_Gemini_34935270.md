Since the terminal flags didn't stop the flicker, the issue likely resides in the Electron Capturer engine within the app itself or a conflict with your hardware's Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) settings. 1. Disable the "Electron Capturer" This is the most effective fix for screen share flickering on Wayland/GNOME. It forces Vesktop to use the system's native PipeWire portal instead of Electron's internal (and often buggy) capture method. Open Vesktop Settings (found at the bottom of the left sidebar, under the Vencord section). Search for "Use Electron Capturer". Toggle this OFF. Restart Vesktop completely for the change to take effect. 2. Disable Hardware Acceleration & Explicit Sync Workarounds If you are on an NVIDIA GPU, GNOME 49's move toward Explicit Sync can cause frames to render out of order, leading to flickering in Electron apps. In-App Toggle: While in Vesktop Settings, find the Hardware Acceleration toggle and turn it OFF. Config File Fix: If the UI is too flickery to navigate, edit your config directly: Open ~/.config/vesktop/settings.json. Change "hardwareAcceleration": true to "hardwareAcceleration": false. Wipe GPU Cache: Corrupt shaders from a CachyOS update can cause persistent artifacts. Run: rm -rf ~/.config/vesktop/GPUCache 3. Check for Adaptive Sync (VRR) Conflicts CachyOS users often encounter heavy flickering when Adaptive Sync is enabled on Wayland, especially during screen sharing where refresh rates may fluctuate. Open GNOME Settings > Displays. Select your primary monitor. If Variable Refresh Rate is set to "Always" or "Automatic," try setting it to Off.

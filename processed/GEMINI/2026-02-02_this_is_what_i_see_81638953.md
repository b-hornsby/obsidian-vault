Your settings are mostly on the right track, but you have one major "blind spot" in your Encoder Settings that is going to make your clips look like a blurry mess in a fast-paced game like Halo. Here is how to tune this for high-level performance and fix the Replay Buffer. 1. Fix the Encoder (The Quality Trap) You are using CBR (Constant Bitrate) at 10,000 Kbps. The Problem: CBR is for streaming. For clipping/recording, it’s a waste of resources. If you stand still, you waste data; if you get into a chaotic firefight in Halo, 10k bitrate will "pixelate" and look terrible. The Fix: Change Rate Control to CQP. The Value: Set the CQ Level between 18 and 22 (18 is higher quality, 22 is smaller files). This tells the GPU: "Maintain this level of visual clarity regardless of how much action is on screen." 2. Activate the Replay Buffer I see the Replay Buffer tab in your screenshot, but it isn't "on" yet. Click the Replay Buffer tab next to "Audio." Check the box "Enable Replay Buffer." Set your time (e.g., 60s or 120s). CRITICAL: Once you click "OK," a new button labeled "Start Replay Buffer" will appear in the bottom right of the main OBS window. You must click this before you start playing, or your hotkey won't do anything. 3. The "Hybrid MP4" Warning I see you've selected Hybrid MP4. The Good: It’s safer than standard MP4 (if OBS crashes, you don't lose the whole file). The Better: Most pro-level Linux users prefer Fragmented MP4 (.mp4) or MKV. MKV is bulletproof. If your PC loses power mid-game, the clip is still saved. You can "Remux" it to MP4 in OBS (File > Remux Recordings) in two seconds. 4. Final Strategic Checklist Audio Track: You only have Track 1 checked. If you want to separate your Mic and Game audio for later editing, you'll need to check Track 2 and assign them in the Advanced Audio Properties. Launch Option: Since you're on NVIDIA (I see the NVENC encoder), make sure you've deleted that Gamescope command. It’s likely what was causing the cursor to disappear and the game to lag. Your Next Step Go to the Hotkeys tab on the left. Find "Save Replay" and set it to a key combo you won't hit by accident (like F10 or Ctrl + Shift + S).

Okay, I see your second question. Question: A technician completes the repair of a printer extruder. What should the technician do to test the repair? You selected: Calibrate the ribbon. The correct answer is Load the filament. Let's break down why and how to remember it: Why you got it wrong & What you should have picked: You chose "Calibrate the ribbon." This option is related to certain types of printers (like thermal transfer or some dot matrix printers), but it's not directly relevant to testing an extruder repair. The key term here is "extruder." An extruder is a component found in 3D printers. Its job is to heat and push (extrude) the printing material. For a 3D printer, the printing material is typically called filament. After repairing the extruder, the logical next step to test if it's working correctly is to load the filament into it and attempt to print, or at least see if the filament can be properly fed through and heated. Let's quickly look at the other options: Activate the fuser: The fuser is a component in laser printers that melts toner onto paper. Not relevant to an extruder. Replace the resin: Resin is used in some types of 3D printers (SLA/DLP), but the term "extruder" specifically points to filament-based 3D printing (FDM). Simple & Memorable Notes: Extruder = 3D Printer Part Extruder Emits Filament. If you fix the "spitter" part of a 3D printer, you need to feed it its "food" (filament) to see if it spits correctly. Memory Tip: Think of an extruder like a fancy, hot glue gun. What do you put into a glue gun to test it? A glue stick (which is like filament). You don't try to calibrate its "ribbon" or activate a "fuser." You load the material it's designed to extrude. Key takeaway: When you see "extruder," immediately think "3D printer" and "filament."

Given the state of the loan, what are my options? It’s getting hard to swing payments and I’m currently looking for a better paying job 

### GEMINI
This is a high-interest situation that is actively draining your resources while you’re in a professional transition. You are roughly 32% of the way through a 75-month term, and your payoff amount ($15,976.59) is actually higher than your principal balance ($15,878.84), which suggests interest is accruing faster than you are currently killing it. Here is the objective breakdown of your options, ranked from strategic to defensive. 1. Refinance (The Strategic Pivot) A 75-month term is unusually long for a used vehicle, which usually means a high APR. The Move: Check with local credit unions in your area. They often offer lower rates than national banks for "used" vehicle refinances. The Reality Check: If your credit score has dipped or your income-to-debt ratio is currently unstable due to the job hunt, you may not qualify for a better rate. However, even extending the term slightly at a lower rate could drop that $385.88 payment to something more manageable while you secure new employment. 2. Private Sale & Downgrade (The "Ego-Check" Option) You owe nearly $16,000. The Move: Research the private party value of a 2020 Honda HR-V. If the car is worth $17k–$18k, you can sell it, pay off the bank, and use the remaining $1k–$2k to buy a "beater" cash car. The Risk: If the car is worth less than $16,000 (negative equity), you would have to pay the bank the difference out of pocket to clear the title. If you don't have that cash, this option is off the table. 3. Deferment/Forbearance (The Defensive Pause) The Move: Call Associated Bank before you miss a payment. Explain that you are between roles and ask for a "hardship extension" or "payment holiday." The Cost: They usually move the missed payments to the end of the loan. Interest still accrues. This is a band-aid, not a cure, but it saves your credit score for 30–60 days while you finish your job search. 4. Voluntary Surrender (The Last Resort) The Reality Check: Do not just let them repossess it. If you absolutely cannot pay, a voluntary surrender looks slightly better on a credit report than a forced repo, but both are catastrophic for your ability to get another loan for years. The Danger: The bank will sell the car at auction for a fraction of its value and sue you for the "deficiency balance"—meaning you’ll have no car and still owe them thousands. Avoid this if at all possible. Immediate Action Plan Verify Equity: Spend 10 minutes on Kelly Blue Book or CarGurus to see what 2020 HR-Vs are selling for. If you have equity, selling is your fastest way to stop the bleeding. Contact the Lender: Don’t wait until 04/20/2026. Call now. Ask specifically about their "Hardship Program." Opportunity Cost: Every hour spent worrying about this loan is an hour not spent on your technical job hunt. If you land a role in IT or System Admin, this $385 becomes a minor line item. If you stay in the current warehouse role, this loan is a mathematical anchor. Are you currently underwater on the car's value, or is there some equity there?

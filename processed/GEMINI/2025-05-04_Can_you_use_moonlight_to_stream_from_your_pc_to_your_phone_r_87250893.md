# Can you use moonlight to stream from your pc to your phone remotely and just use the backbone as a controller and if you really wanted to, airdrop that to the tv for a “docked” experience? 

### USER
Can you use moonlight to stream from your pc to your phone remotely and just use the backbone as a controller and if you really wanted to, airdrop that to the tv for a “docked” experience? 

### GEMINI
That's an interesting idea! Let's break it down: 1. Streaming with Moonlight and Backbone: Yes, you can definitely use Moonlight to stream from your PC to your phone remotely. Moonlight is designed for this purpose, allowing you to play your PC games on various devices over a network connection. And yes, the Backbone One controller should work perfectly with Moonlight on your phone. Moonlight supports various Bluetooth controllers, including those designed for mobile gaming like the Backbone. This would give you a comfortable and familiar gaming experience. 2. "Docked" Experience with AirPlay: If you have an iPhone and an Apple TV or a smart TV that supports AirPlay 2, then in theory, you could AirPlay mirror your iPhone's screen to the TV. This would give you a larger display for your game. However, keep in mind a few potential drawbacks: Latency: Introducing an additional step like AirPlay mirroring could introduce noticeable latency (delay) between your input on the Backbone and what you see on the TV. This could negatively impact your gaming experience, especially for fast-paced games. Video Quality: Depending on your network conditions and the capabilities of your devices, the video quality might be reduced when mirroring via AirPlay. Aspect Ratio/Scaling: The aspect ratio of your phone screen might not perfectly match your TV, potentially leading to black bars or stretching of the image. In summary: You can achieve the core functionality of remotely streaming your PC games to your phone and using the Backbone as a controller with Moonlight. The "docked" experience by AirPlaying to a TV is technically possible, but it might come with compromises in terms of latency and video quality. It would be something you'd need to test to see if it provides a satisfactory experience for the games you play.

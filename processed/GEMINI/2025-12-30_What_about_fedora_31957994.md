# What about fedora?

### USER
What about fedora?

### GEMINI
Fedora is the "Professional’s Playground." It’s polished, stable, and backed by Red Hat. If CachyOS is a tuned racing car, Fedora is a high-end BMW. It’s fast, reliable, and comfortable, but it won't let you ignore the rules. Since you have an i7-12700K and an RTX 3070, here is the unfiltered truth about choosing Fedora in late 2025: 1. The "NVIDIA Headache" (The Fedora Way) Fedora has a "Free Software" philosophy. Unlike CachyOS, it will not include NVIDIA drivers on the ISO. The Reality: You will boot into a generic "Nouveau" driver that will make your 3070 feel like a GPU from 2010. The Fix: You must manually enable the "RPM Fusion" repositories to get the proprietary drivers. If you don't do this correctly, or if you have Secure Boot enabled without signing the drivers, you will boot into a black screen. It is a "rite of passage" for Fedora users. 2. Performance: Fedora vs. CachyOS CPU: Your i7-12700K has P-cores and E-cores. Fedora’s kernel is very modern (currently 6.14+), so it handles Intel Thread Director well. However, it is not "aggressive." GPU: Gaming performance on Fedora is excellent, but CachyOS will consistently beat it by 3–8% because CachyOS uses optimized binaries ($x86-64-v3$) that Fedora avoids for the sake of broad compatibility. 3. GNOME vs. KDE on Fedora Fedora Workstation (GNOME): This is the flagship experience. It is the closest thing to a "Mac" experience on Linux. It is incredibly smooth, but Fedora is pushing Wayland-only. With an RTX 3070, this is finally stable (thanks to Explicit Sync), but some older games might still flicker. Fedora KDE Spin: Also excellent, and as we discussed, more "customizable" later. The Advisor’s Verdict: Fedora is for you IF... You want a system that feels "official" and corporate-backed. You don't mind spending 20 minutes setting up repositories (RPM Fusion) before you can actually game. You want the best GNOME experience in the world. The Advisor’s Verdict: Stay away from Fedora IF... You want the absolute maximum FPS out of your 3070. You want a "one-click" gaming setup. You want to use Hyprland (Fedora’s Hyprland support is "barebones" compared to CachyOS). Your Prioritized Strategy (If you choose Fedora) Download Fedora 41 (or 42 if released). BIOS: Disable Secure Boot. Fedora can work with it, but for an NVIDIA user, it adds a layer of complexity (MOK signing) that is a massive waste of time. The "First 5 Minutes" Command: After installing, you must run this to make your GPU work: sudo dnf install akmod-nvidia xorg-x11-drv-nvidia-cuda Mac-ification: Install gnome-tweaks and extension-manager. Search for the "WhiteSur" GTK theme and "Dash to Dock". Which way are you leaning? Does the "Rock-solid/Professional" vibe of Fedora appeal to you more than the "Optimized/Gamer-centric" speed of CachyOS? I can provide a "First-Hour Checklist" for either one to make sure your i7-12700K isn't idling on the wrong cores.

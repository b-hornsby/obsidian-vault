# What if the amount and everything is correct and I am technically at fault?

### USER
What if the amount and everything is correct and I am technically at fault?

### GEMINI
Even if you are "technically at fault," the legal system doesn't automatically hand them your money. You still have rights, and the plaintiff (the debt collector) still has a burden of proof. Here is the cold, strategic truth of your situation: 1. Admission is a Strategy, Not Just a Fact If you admit everything and do nothing, the court enters a Default Judgment. This gives the law firm (Zwicker & Associates) "superpowers" they don't currently have, such as: Wage Garnishment: They can take up to 25% of your paycheck directly from your employer. Bank Levies: They can freeze your bank account and take the balance. Judgment Liens: They can put a lien on your property or assets. You want to avoid a judgment at all costs. 2. The "Answer" Buys You Time Even if the amount is 100% correct, you should still file an Answer. Why? Because it moves the case from "Automatic Win" for them to "Contested Case." It buys you months of time. It forces the law firm to actually work for the money, which makes them much more likely to settle for a lower amount just to close the file. 3. Your Move: The "Pre-Judgment Settlement" Since you know the debt is yours, your best path is to negotiate a settlement before a judge signs a judgment. Lump Sum: If you owe $5,000, they might take $2,500–$3,000 right now to walk away. Payment Plan: You can offer to pay $100/month. If they agree, they will usually "stay" (pause) the lawsuit as long as you keep paying. Crucial Rule: Never agree to a payment plan over the phone without getting a written "Stipulation of Settlement" first. If you pay them without a signed contract, they can still take a default judgment against you. 4. Check for "Standing" In many cases, the original bank (like Chase or Citibank) sold your debt to a third party. Even if you owe the money to the bank, the debt collector suing you must prove they legally own the right to sue you. If they can't produce the "Assignment of Debt" (the receipt showing they bought your specific account), the case can be dismissed—even if you "technically" owe the money. Your Immediate Plan File the Answer anyway. Use "Deny for lack of knowledge" for the technical paragraphs about their ownership of the debt. This keeps the door open. Contact a Consumer Attorney. Since you are in Akron, call Community Legal Aid (330-535-4191). Tell them: "I've been sued in Stow Municipal Court. I admit the debt is mine, but I need help negotiating a settlement to avoid garnishment." Calculate your "Walk-Away" Number. What is the absolute maximum monthly payment you can afford without missing rent or food? Don't offer a penny more than that. Would you like me to help you draft the specific language for your "Answer" so you can get it filed at the courthouse tomorrow?

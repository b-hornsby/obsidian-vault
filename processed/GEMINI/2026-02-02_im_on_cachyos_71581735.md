### GEMINI
If you’re on CachyOS, you’re on a performance-tuned, Arch-based system. That means there is absolutely no excuse for having "mystery meat" icons cluttering your desktop. CachyOS users are expected to know exactly what is running on their kernel. This is likely a Flatpak, a system package (pacman), or a desktop entry left over from a botched uninstallation. 1. Identify the Source Open your terminal. We aren't guessing; we're hunting. Check Pacman (System Packages): pacman -Qs video player Look for names like celluloid, mpv, or vlc. If you see something generic you don't recognize, that's your target. Check Flatpak: flatpak list | grep -i video Flatpaks are notorious for adding generic icons to your menu. 2. The Kill Command Once you’ve identified the package name, execute it with zero sentimentality. If it’s a Pacman package: sudo pacman -Rs [package_name] (The -s flag removes dependencies that aren't needed by anything else. Don't leave junk behind.) If it’s a Flatpak: flatpak uninstall [application_id] Follow this with flatpak uninstall --unused to scrub the leftover runtimes. 3. The "Ghost Icon" Scenario If you’ve uninstalled the app but the icon is still mocking you, you have a lingering .desktop file. This is a failure of system maintenance. Navigate to your local applications folder: cd ~/.local/share/applications/ Search for the offender: ls | grep -i video Delete it: rm [filename].desktop Note: If it's not there, check the system-wide directory: /usr/share/applications/. 4. The Mirror: Why is this here? CachyOS is built for speed and optimization. By having unidentified "Video Player" apps on your system, you are actively defeating the purpose of using a high-performance distro. Bloat is Friction: Every unnecessary entry in your app drawer is a mental tax. The Assumption: You likely installed a meta-package or a "suggested" suite during the CachyOS install (like a specific desktop environment's 'extra' or 'goodies' group). The Correction: Stop mass-installing "suites." Install exactly what you need, package by package. Use mpv if you want performance; get rid of everything else. Would you like the specific terminal commands to audit your entire package list for other "bloat" categories you've likely ignored?

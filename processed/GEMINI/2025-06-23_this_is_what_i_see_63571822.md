Thanks for sharing the screenshot! That's very clear. You've found the "Train LoRA" section within the "Training" tab, and indeed, it has a "Raw text file" option. However, it's very important to understand that what you're seeing here is for Fine-Tuning a LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for your model, not for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). Here's the crucial difference: LoRA Training (what you're seeing): You provide a "Raw text file" as a dataset. When you click "Start LoRA Training," the model will learn from this text and adjust its internal parameters (weights). This makes the model better at generating text in the style or knowledge domain of your provided documents. This is a permanent change to the model's behavior. It's resource-intensive (requires significant GPU power and time, often hours to days). It doesn't provide specific citations or directly retrieve chunks of text. If you add new documents later, you'd have to retrain. RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation - what we want): You provide documents (like your .txt files) to a separate "vector store" or "knowledge base." When you ask a question, the system first searches your documents for the most relevant information in real-time. It then takes those relevant snippets and feeds them into the model's prompt as context. The model then generates an answer based on that context. This does NOT change the model's internal parameters. It's fast (seconds) and less resource-intensive during inference. It allows for dynamic updates (just add/remove documents, re-index) and often provides citations (showing which document the info came from). Why this "Training" option isn't suitable for your help desk RAG goal: No Direct Retrieval/Citation: It won't look up specific answers from your documents and present them, nor will it tell you which document the answer came from. It simply integrates the knowledge from your documents into the model's general understanding. Resource Intensive: Running LoRA training requires a strong GPU and a lot of time, which isn't practical for simply making documents available for lookup. Updates are Hard: If you add a new troubleshooting tip, you'd have to retrain the LoRA, which is a lengthy process each time. Conclusion: Given that the dedicated RAG extensions are not appearing in your "Settings" tab, and this "Training" option is for fine-tuning, it seems your current Oobabooga build might not have the integrated RAG features readily available through the UI as we expected. This is a bit of a roadblock for achieving RAG directly within your current Oobabooga UI. What are your options now? Consider a Fresh Oobabooga Installation: This is the most reliable way to ensure you have the latest code and all standard extensions. It would involve backing up your user_data/models and data_sources folders, and then re-cloning the Oobabooga repository. We could guide you through this, but it's a more involved step. Explore External RAG (More Advanced): You could set up a RAG system outside of Oobabooga (e.g., using Python libraries like LangChain and a vector database like FAISS or ChromaDB). This system would: Ingest your data_sources files. Take a user's question. Find relevant chunks from your documents. Then, it would send those relevant chunks along with the user's question to your Oobabooga model (via its API) for the model to generate a final answer. This is more complex to set up but gives you ultimate control and is how many advanced RAG systems operate. What would you prefer to try next? We can either investigate a fresh Oobabooga installation (which is often the quickest way to fix oddities like missing extensions) or look into external RAG if you're feeling adventurous.

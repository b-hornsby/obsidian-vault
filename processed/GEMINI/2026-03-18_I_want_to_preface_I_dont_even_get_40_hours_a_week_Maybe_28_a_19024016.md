This is exactly why that Form 20 is so important. If you’re only getting 28 hours at $11/hr, your gross monthly income is actually around $1,330 (28 hrs x $11 x 4.33 weeks). In the eyes of the court, that puts you well below the poverty line. You are exactly who this fee waiver was designed for. Updated Math for Form 20 (Page 2) Use these numbers instead of the ones we discussed earlier: Gross Monthly Employment Income: Write $1,330. Total Monthly Income: Write $1,330. Monthly Expenses: When you list your rent, food, gas, and phone, that total is likely going to be higher than $1,330. Don't panic: It is perfectly fine if your expenses are higher than your income. It shows the court you are currently struggling and definitely cannot afford to pay them a filing fee. How to Answer the "I, the Defendant, answer..." (Image 1) Since you are under-employed and fighting an $8,000 lawsuit, you need to be very clear on those blank lines in the Answer form. Write this: "Defendant admits his name and address." "Defendant denies the allegations in the Complaint and is without knowledge or information sufficient to form a belief as to the truth of the remaining allegations." "Defendant specifically denies the accuracy of the alleged balance and demands strict proof of the chain of ownership of the debt." I Ask the Court to: "Dismiss the Complaint at Plaintiff's cost, and for any other relief the Court deems just." Final "Truth Mirror" Check You are in a tight spot, but you are handling this like a professional. The Law Firm: They are counting on you to be too overwhelmed to show up. The Reality: By filing these papers tomorrow, you are telling them, "I'm still here, I'm fighting, and I'm officially 'Judgment Proof' because of my income." This makes you a massive headache for them, which is exactly where you want to be when it comes time to settle this later for $500 instead of $8,000. Do you have all the specific amounts for your rent, gas, and phone bills ready to write into those boxes now?

### GEMINI
You’ve hit on the exact reason most people stay stuck in jobs they hate. Most people use their headline as a label. You are using yours as a destination. If you put "Material Handler" as your headline, the only people who will call you are warehouse managers looking for someone to move boxes. You have to decide: do you want a headline that describes your past, or a headline that qualifies you for your future? Why your headline "not fitting" is actually your biggest advantage: The "Passive Candidate" Illusion: When a recruiter sees your headline, they don't know your current paycheck comes from a warehouse. They see "IT Operations | Linux (Arch/CachyOS)." They assume you are already doing that work somewhere and might be open to a new role. This gives you leverage. It’s much easier to get hired for a tech job when you look like a tech person. The "Pattern Interrupt": Recruiters scan hundreds of profiles that say "Entry Level IT" or "Unemployed." Your headline is dense with high-level technical specifics. It forces them to stop and think, "Wait, what is this guy's deal?" That "pause" is how you get the interview. LinkedIn is a Search Engine, not a Resume: Recruiters don't search for "People who currently work in warehouses." They search for "Linux," "Technical Support," and "Automation." If those words aren't in your headline, you are invisible to the people who can actually get you out of the warehouse. The Strategy Check If you are worried about being "dishonest" because it isn't your current job title, don't be. A headline is meant to define your area of expertise. You do manage Linux (Arch/CachyOS). You do build AI Automation. You do understand IT Operations. You aren't lying; you are finally accurately describing the value you bring to a company, rather than the manual labor you perform for your current employer. The Prioritized Plan: To pull this off, your Experience section (the part under your current job) needs to act as the "Receipts." Instead of listing warehouse tasks, we frame them as Operational Systems: Inventory Management: "Maintained data integrity for high-volume logistics systems." Efficiency: "Optimized workflow throughput in a high-pressure environment." Reliability: "8-year tenure with consistent performance in mission-critical logistics." Would you like me to rewrite your warehouse experience bullets right now so they actually align with your "IT Operations" headline? (Just tell me the 3 most common things you do in a day, and I'll translate them into "Tech-Speak.")

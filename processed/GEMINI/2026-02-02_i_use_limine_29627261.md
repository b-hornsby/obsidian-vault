### GEMINI
Using Limine on CachyOS makes this slightly different because Limine doesn't use the standard /etc/default/grub path. However, CachyOS provides a specific way to manage these parameters so they don't get wiped during a kernel update. If the Yeti still requires a "physical reset" (unplugging) even after our BIOS and software tweaks, we must apply a Kernel Quirk. This tells the Linux kernel at the lowest possible level to skip the power-management handshakes that are likely crashing the Yeti's internal firmware. 1. Apply the Kernel Quirk via Limine In CachyOS, the source for Limine's kernel parameters is typically /etc/default/limine. Open the file: sudo nano /etc/default/limine Find the line starting with KERNEL_CMDLINE[default]. Add the quirk at the end of the existing parameters (inside the quotes): usbcore.quirks=046d:0ab7:k Note: The k flag disables Link Power Management (LPM) for this device, which is the #1 killer of Blue Yetis on Linux. Regenerate the Limine config: sudo limine-update (If that command isn't found, try sudo limine-mkinitcpio or check the CachyOS wiki for your specific installation's update command—usually limine-update on CachyOS). 2. The "Nuclear" BIOS Toggle Since you are still having issues, there is a conflict between your motherboard's "Soft Off" power and the Yeti's "Sleep" state. Go back to BIOS. Find: Advanced -> Onboard Devices Configuration. Change: USB power delivery in Soft Off state (S5) to DISABLED. Why? By disabling this, the Yeti will die completely when you turn off the PC. When you turn it back on, it is forced to do a "Cold Boot" alongside the system. Currently, it's staying in a "warm" zombie state that is confusing the Linux handshake. 3. Move the Hardware (Bus Isolation) I noticed in your lsusb that you have a FiiO DAC, a Webcam, and a Stream Deck all on Bus 001. The Yeti is isochronous (it needs perfectly timed data). Your webcam is also isochronous. If they are on the same bus, the controller will often drop one to save the other during the boot-up initialization surge. Action: Plug your FiiO DAC or Webcam into a Blue or Red USB port (these usually belong to the empty Bus 002 we saw in your log). Leave the Yeti in a Black USB 2.0 port by itself. The Reality Check If you apply the usbcore.quirks and disable S5 power in BIOS, and it still doesn't work, then the Yeti's internal capacitor is likely failing to discharge fast enough to reset the firmware between reboots. Try the Kernel Quirk first. If you reboot and wpctl status shows the Yeti without you touching the cable, you’ve won. Would you like me to check if the quirk successfully applied after you reboot? (Just run cat /proc/cmdline and paste it here).

Since you're brainstorming, let’s look at the Hermes v0.6 "Role-Based" capabilities. In 2026, the trend has shifted from "chatting" to "Pipeline Execution." Because you’re running this in a Docker cage, you can give Hermes specialized "Profiles" that act as independent employees. Here’s what you can actually do with this setup: 1. The "Ghost Writer" for Technical Content Since you're a streamer and a hardware enthusiast, you can have a profile that watches your own activity. The Workflow: Hermes monitors your Skills/ folder in Obsidian. When you document a new fix for Tiny11 or a GPU overclock, Hermes automatically drafts a technical blog post or a script for a TikTok/Twitch segment. The Hook: It uses your "Identity" file to make sure the tone matches your personality—no generic "AI-speak." 2. The "DeFi Risk Officer" Since you're interested in the Solana ecosystem and self-custody: The Workflow: Hermes uses its browser tool (inside the Docker sandbox) to scan decentralized news feeds and GitHub commits for the protocols you use (like Phantom or Ledger updates). The Action: If it detects a major vulnerability or a "rug pull" pattern in a project you've mentioned in your notes, it sends an Emergency Telegram Alert: "Warning: Protocol X just had a suspicious liquidity drain. You have [X] amount stored there according to your October logs. Recommend moving to cold storage." 3. The "Logistics-to-IT" Career Pivot Engine This is the most practical use for your current situation. The Workflow: Hermes acts as a Headhunter. It scans the Akron/Cleveland job market not just for "IT Support," but for "Logistics Tech" roles that specifically need your 8-year background in high-volume environments. The Move: It identifies companies using outdated warehouse management systems (WMS) and drafts a proposal for how you could migrate them to a local, AI-enhanced system (like the one you're building right now). It turns your "Job Hunt" into a "Consulting Pitch." 4. The "Automated Home Lab" Manager Since you're running Tiny11 and CachyOS: The Workflow: Hermes can manage your system maintenance. It can check for driver updates, monitor your RTX 3070 temps during long AI runs, and even suggest BIOS tweaks based on your Asus Prime Z790-P manual (which you've ingested into your library). The Action: It appends a "System Health Report" to your Obsidian vault every morning. 5. The "Second Me" Digital Twin (The Mirror) The Workflow: Because it has read your Gemini/Claude history, it knows your "blind spots." The Daily Nudge: You can set a cron-job (timer) in Docker for Hermes to message you at 9:00 AM: "You said you were going to finish the Docker setup today. Last time you reached this stage in a project, you got distracted by a new Linux distro and quit. Don't do that today. Get to the desk." The "Why" for the Docker Cage Without Docker, giving an AI the power to "scan your system" or "monitor your crypto" is a massive security risk. With Docker, you are giving the agent a "view-only" window into your life. It can see what you allow, and it can talk to the outside world (Telegram) without ever having the keys to your actual Windows login. The Bottom Line: You're building a system that allows you to stop being the "Operator" and start being the "Architect." What's the one thing in your daily routine right now that you hate doing manually? That’s what we should give to Hermes first.

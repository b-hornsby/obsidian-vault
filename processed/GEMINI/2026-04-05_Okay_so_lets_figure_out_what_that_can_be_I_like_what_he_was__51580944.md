"Above average" with AI tools is a dangerous middle ground. It’s enough to be dangerous, but not enough to be disciplined. To move from "dabbling" to a $4,000 service, you have to stop playing with the tech and start solving a high-stakes problem. Looking at what you’ve actually built, you have two clear paths. One is a "nice to have," and the other is "mission critical." Guess which one pays $4,000? The Two Products in Your Portfolio Option 1: The "Immortal Agent" (Web3/DeFi Automation) You’ve already worked on "Operation Immortal Agent"—connecting a "brain" (Claude) to "hands" (Phantom MCP). The Value: People in crypto have more money than time. Managing yield, monitoring rugs, and rebalancing pools is a 24/7 job. The $4k Sell: You aren't selling a "bot." You are selling "Automated Portfolio Defense." You build custom agentic setups for high-net-worth individuals to automate their specific DeFi strategies. Why you feel like a larp: Because if the bot fails, they lose money. You need to be rock-solid on security and self-custody logic. Option 2: The "Airgapped Architect" (Local/Offline IT Support) You’ve engineered a local IT helpdesk using CrewAI and local LLMs. The Value: Privacy and security. There are small law firms, medical offices, or manufacturing plants (like the warehouse environments you know) that cannot send their data to OpenAI but need AI efficiency. The $4k Sell: You sell a "Privacy-First AI Employee." You set up a local server (Ollama/LM Studio), configure the agents (CrewAI), and give them a tool that answers proprietary company questions without hitting the cloud. Why you feel like a larp: Because you’re a "beginner" at coding. But remember: They aren't hiring a Senior Dev; they are hiring a System Integrator. The Brutal Truth on "Vibe Coding" Vibe coding (Cursor/Windsurf) is great for generating code, but it’s terrible at architecting systems. If you want to sell a $4,000 service: Stop saying "Vibe Coding" to clients. It sounds like you're guessing. Call it "Agentic System Design." Focus on the "Hands," not just the "Brain." Anyone can talk to ChatGPT. Not everyone can link an LLM to an MCP, a local database, or a hardware wallet. The integration is the service. The Opportunity Cost Decision You are in a financial hardship. You don't have time to "dalliance" with both. Path A (Crypto): Higher upside, faster cash, but higher stress and potential for legal/technical blowback if things go south. Path B (Local IT): Slower burn, requires more "boots on the ground" sales, but builds a sustainable, professional reputation in your local Akron market. Based on your current skill set, which of those two projects felt more "automated" and less like you were babysitting it? That’s the one we turn into a business.

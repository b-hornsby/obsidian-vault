### GEMINI
"Full sending" is the only way to do it if you actually want to learn. Half-measures like dual-booting just give you an escape hatch that keeps you from solving the "Linux way" of doing things. Since you’re nuking a 2TB drive that had Windows 11 on it, here is the high-level reality check and the tactical plan to ensure you don't end up with a bricked bootloader or wasted space. 1. The Reality Check (Blind Spots) BitLocker is your first hurdle: If Windows 11 was encrypted with BitLocker (standard on most modern installs), the Linux installer might see the drive as "busy" or "locked." Correction: If you haven't wiped it yet, disable BitLocker in Windows first. If you've already booted the USB, you'll likely need to use blkdiscard or gparted from within the Live environment to "force" the drive open. The "Replace Partition" Trap: Don't just click "Replace a partition." Windows leaves behind tiny recovery and EFI partitions that will clutter your 2TB drive. Direct Advice: Choose "Erase Disk." This wipes the partition table entirely and lets CachyOS handle the alignment for its optimized kernels. The Filesystem Choice: CachyOS defaults to XFS or BTRFS. If you want the "next level" experience, go BTRFS. It allows for instant snapshots. If you break something (and you will), you can roll back the entire OS in seconds from the boot menu. 2. The Execution Plan Step A: BIOS/UEFI Prep Disable Secure Boot: CachyOS supports it, but it’s a manual headache to enroll keys. Turn it off to save yourself two hours of troubleshooting on your first day. SATA Mode: Ensure your storage controller is set to AHCI, not RAID/RST. Windows often ships with RST, which Linux handles poorly. Step B: The Installation Boot the ISO: Choose the "proprietary" or "NVIDIA" driver boot option if you have an NVIDIA card. Launch the Installer: * Select Erase Disk. Swap: Select "Swap to File" or "Swap with Hibernation." Since you have a 2TB drive, don't be stingy—give it 16GB–32GB of swap space so your system doesn't choke under heavy loads. Bootloader: Choose systemd-boot. It is cleaner, faster, and more modern than GRUB. The "Full Send" Selection: When it asks for packages, don't bloat the system. Pick your Desktop Environment (KDE Plasma is the gold standard for performance on Cachy), and check the CachyOS Gaming meta-package if you plan to play games. Step C: The First Boot Update immediately: Open a terminal and run cachyos-rate-mirrors followed by sudo pacman -Syu. The "Mirror" Test: If you find yourself looking for a .exe, stop. Open the CachyOS Hello app or use pacman. 3. Opportunity Cost Warning You are moving to an Arch-based distro. The "cost" is that you are now the primary maintainer. If you don't read the terminal output when updating, you'll eventually break a dependency. Your Priority Action: Once installed, open the CachyOS Hello app and set up Snapper (for BTRFS snapshots). This is your "Undo" button. Without it, your "full send" could end in a "full reinstall" if you tinker too hard.

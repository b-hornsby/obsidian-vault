# Now what do I do with the form 20?

### USER
Now what do I do with the form 20?

### GEMINI
The Form 20 (Civil Fee Waiver Affidavit) is your financial shield. Because you make $11/hour, you are legally entitled to have your court costs waived in Ohio, but you have to file this form correctly to trigger that protection. Here is the exact play-by-play for the Form 20: 1. Fill it out (But DON’T sign) The "Header": Copy the Plaintiff, Defendant, and Case Number exactly as they appear on your Answer. Public Benefits: If you get SNAP (Food Stamps) or Medicaid, check those boxes. At $11/hr, this makes the waiver almost automatic. Income & Expenses: Be honest. List your $11/hr wages and all your bills (rent, phone, gas). The goal is to show the court that your expenses eat up your income. The Signature Line: Leave this blank. You must sign it in front of the Clerk at the courthouse. 2. Take it to the "Civil Clerk" window When you go to the Stow Municipal Court (4400 Courthouse Blvd), head to the Civil Division. The Request: Hand the Clerk your Answer and your Form 20. The Notary: Say: "I need to file this Answer, and I have a Form 20 Fee Waiver that I need to have notarized and filed as well." * The ID: The Clerk will ask for your ID, watch you sign the Form 20, and then they will stamp/seal it. This service is free. 3. What this does for you The $0 Filing: While it's already free to file an Answer in Stow, this waiver covers you for future costs. If the bank tries to set a hearing or if you need to file a motion later, you won't be hit with a $50–$100 fee. The "Broke" Signal: More importantly, it puts a sworn statement in the official court record that you are low-income. When the bank’s lawyer sees a "Fee Waiver Granted" entry on the docket, they realize they are suing someone who is "judgment proof." This often makes them much more willing to settle for a tiny amount later. Final Logistics Print 1 Copy of Form 20. (You don't need 3 of this one, just the original for the court). Print 3 Copies of your Answer. Go to the Court tomorrow. You are beating the deadline and playing this perfectly. Once you leave the courthouse with your stamped copies, the "timer" on your 28 days stops.

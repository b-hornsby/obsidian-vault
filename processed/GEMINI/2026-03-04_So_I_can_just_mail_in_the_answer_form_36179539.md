# So I can just mail in the answer form?

### USER
So I can just mail in the answer form?

### GEMINI
Yes, you can mail it, but there is a specific way you have to do it to ensure the court actually accepts it. If you just drop it in a blue USPS mailbox and hope for the best, you risk a default judgment if it gets lost or delayed. Here is the "bulletproof" way to file your answer by mail in Akron. 1. The Mailing Strategy Do not use standard stamps. Go to the post office counter and send both envelopes (the one to the Court and the one to the Attorney) via Certified Mail, Return Receipt Requested. Why? You will get a green card back in the mail signed by the court clerk. This is your "receipt" and legal proof that you met your 28-day deadline. 2. Where to Send Them You must send your Answer to two different places: A. To the Court (The "Filing"): Akron Municipal Court Attn: Clerk of Courts, Civil Division 172 S. Broadway St., Suite 100 Akron, OH 44308 B. To the Plaintiff's Lawyer (The "Service"): Look at the Summons you uploaded. The lawyer's name and address are listed at the bottom or on the first page of the Complaint. You must mail them a copy as well. 3. The "Certificate of Service" This is the part people usually miss. At the very bottom of your Answer form, there is a section called the Certificate of Service. You must fill this out before you mail it. It should say something like: "I hereby certify that a copy of this Answer was served upon the Plaintiff's attorney [Attorney Name] at [Attorney Address] by ordinary U.S. Mail on this [Date] day of [Month], 2026." 4. Include the Filing Fee (If Applicable) While many "Answers" are free to file, some courts charge a small "convenience" or "security" fee. Action: Call the Akron Civil Clerk at (330) 375-2920 tomorrow morning. Ask: "Is there a filing fee for an Answer in case number [Your Case Number]?" * If there is, you’ll need to include a Money Order (never cash) made out to "Akron Municipal Court." The "Brutally Honest" Reality Check Mailing is the "risky" route. If the post office loses it, the court won't care; they'll just see that nothing was filed and let the Plaintiff win. The Pro Move: If you are physically able, take it to 172 S. Broadway St. in person. * Walk to the Civil Clerk's window (Suite 100). Hand them the original and a copy. Ask them to "Time-Stamp" your copy. Result: You walk out with a stamped document that is 100% proof you are safe from a default judgment.

# Cursor pro or windsurf?

### USER
Cursor pro or windsurf?

### GEMINI
Here is the brutally honest breakdown. You are splitting hairs between two excellent tools, but they cater to two very different psychological profiles. The Short Answer Pick Cursor if you want to be the Captain (you drive, it assists). Pick Windsurf if you want a Chauffeur (you tell it the destination, it drives). If your goal is still "saving money," Windsurf is cheaper ($15/mo vs. Cursor's $20/mo) and arguably offers better value for a "set it and forget it" workflow. 1. The Core Difference (It’s Not Just Features) Cursor (The Power Tool) Philosophy: "I am an extension of your hands." Best Feature: Tab (Copilot++). It predicts your next edit with terrifying accuracy. It feels like telepathy. Weakness: You have to manage the context manually. You constantly have to hit @Codebase or @File to tell it what to look at. If you don't guide it, it gets stupid. Who it's for: Developers who know exactly what they want to write but want to write it 10x faster. Windsurf (The Agent) Philosophy: "I am an employee sitting next to you." Best Feature: Cascade. It has a deeper, more "passive" understanding of your project structure. You don't have to constantly tag files; it just knows where things are because it indexes your code more aggressively effectively. Weakness: It can feel slower and sometimes "over-engineers" simple fixes because it's trying to think for you. Who it's for: "Vibe coders" (people building apps who aren't expert coders) or developers who want to delegate entire tasks ("Fix this bug") rather than just speed up typing. 2. The Financial Reality You were worried about the $20/mo. Cursor: $20/month. Hard stop. (Free tier exists but runs out of "fast" requests quickly). Windsurf: $15/month for the Pro plan. The "Hidden" Cost: Both tools essentially resell you Claude 3.5 Sonnet with a wrapper. Cursor's UI is slightly more "janky" (it's a hard fork of VS Code that sometimes feels cluttered). Windsurf's UI is polished and feels like a native evolution of VS Code, but the ecosystem of plugins is identical (since both are VS Code forks). 3. My Recommendation If you are a beginner or "Indie Hacker": Get Windsurf. It is cheaper ($15 vs $20). Its "Flow" system is much better at handling vague instructions like "Make the login page look modern" without you needing to specify which CSS files to edit. It requires less cognitive load to operate. If you are a pro developer (Senior Engineer, etc.): Get Cursor. The Tab autocomplete feature is superior to Windsurf's equivalent. When you are deep in a file, Cursor predicts the logic you are about to type, not just the syntax. You will get frustrated by Windsurf trying to "hold your hand" when you just want to blast out code. Next Step: Since Windsurf has a lower barrier to entry and better "out of the box" context awareness, install Windsurf first. Use their free trial. If you find yourself annoyed that it's taking too much control, switch to Cursor.

# I already got some firm that texted me about this. What should I do?

### USER
I already got some firm that texted me about this. What should I do?

### GEMINI
It’s common for law firms to monitor court filings and "scrape" public records to find new defendants. They often reach out immediately via text or mail to secure you as a client before you’ve even had a chance to process the summons. However, you need to handle these messages with extreme caution. Here is the strategy: 1. Verify the Firm (Do Not Click Links) Scammers often impersonate law firms or use generic names like "Legal Assistance Center" to trick people. Search the name: Look up the law firm on an independent search engine. Check the Ohio Bar: Ensure the person contacting you is a licensed attorney in good standing. You can use the Supreme Court of Ohio Attorney Search. Check the Phone Number: If the text came from a personal-looking number or an "automated" short-code, be wary. 2. Spot the Red Flags In Ohio, lawyers must follow strict ethical rules (Rule 7.3) when texting potential clients. It is a major red flag if: The message doesn't start and end with "ADVERTISING MATERIAL" or "ADVERTISEMENT ONLY." They don't explain how they got your information. They use high-pressure tactics or "guarantee" a win. They ask for payment (via wire, gift card, or crypto) or sensitive info (SSN) immediately. 3. The "Solicitation" Reality While legal in Ohio, these firms are essentially "ambulance chasers" for civil suits. They are looking for volume. The Opportunity Cost: By going with the first firm that texts you, you might be hiring a "mill" that won't give your case personal attention. Your Move: You are the "buyer" here. You do not owe them a response. If you want a lawyer, you should interview 2–3 different ones—some who didn't text you—to compare fees and strategy. 4. Direct Response If you aren't interested, do not reply. Replying "Stop" or "Who is this?" confirms your number is active and might lead to more solicitations. If you are interested, call their official office number found on their website, not the number that texted you. Your Immediate Priority The law firm texting you is a distraction. Your real problem is the 28-day deadline mentioned in the summons. Would you like me to help you find the specific "Answer" form for the Akron Municipal Court or the Summit County Court of Common Pleas so you can see what you're up against?

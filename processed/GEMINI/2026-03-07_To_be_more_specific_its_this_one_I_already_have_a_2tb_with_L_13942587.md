### GEMINI
I understand you are looking at this from a budget-preservation perspective given the "SSD Apocalypse" price hikes of 2026. However, using that Seagate BarraCuda 1TB 7200RPM HDD for a modern Windows dual-boot—specifically for titles like Call of Duty and Fortnite—is a strategic error that will make your high-end hardware feel broken. 1. The Reality for Modern Shooters (CoD, Fortnite) While you want to save money, these games are no longer designed for mechanical drives: Loading Times: A 7200RPM HDD like yours typically takes 30–60+ seconds to load a level, whereas even a basic NVMe SSD does it in 5–10 seconds. Competitive Disadvantage: In games like Call of Duty, the slowest loader in a party can delay the start for everyone. Worse, if you don't load into the match before it starts, you miss the initial deployment or strategic positioning. Stuttering and Pop-in: High-fidelity games stream assets constantly. On an HDD, you will likely experience texture pop-in (blurry objects) and temporary stuttering during intense action because the drive can't keep up with the data requests of your i7-12700K and RTX 3070. 2. The 2026 Price Crisis vs. Value You mentioned "crazy price hikes," and you aren't wrong—NAND flash costs have surged due to AI demand consuming 70% of global memory. However, the opportunity cost of hobbling your machine is higher. The Bottom Line: You are running a Z790 motherboard and DDR5 RAM. Using a drive that maxes out at ~190 MB/s when your board supports 7,000+ MB/s is a massive bottleneck. 3. Recommended Budget Strategy If a high-end 2TB drive is out of reach, do not settle for the HDD. Instead, look for "entry-level" NVMe drives that still vastly outperform any HDD: The "Small Boot" Compromise: Buy a 500GB or 1TB budget Gen4 drive like the Crucial P310 or Kingston NV3. These are often priced more competitively even during shortages. The Cold Storage Pivot: Move your Linux OS or non-essential files to that 1TB HDD and use it strictly for backups or media storage. Windows and your active games must be on an SSD to maintain the "brutally honest" standard of performance your specs deserve. The Verdict: Do not install Windows or modern AAA games on that BarraCuda. It will turn your gaming experience into a frustrating series of loading screens and micro-stutters.

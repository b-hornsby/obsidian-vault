Not at Ken Ganley, not at Great Lakes Honda, and not even at most "reputable" special finance dealers. They generally require a minimum of $1,500 to $1,800 a month in provable gross income just to open a file. You are currently spending 34% of your entire gross income on a single car payment. When you add gas and insurance, you are likely working 3 out of 4 weeks every month just to pay for the Honda. The Objective Truth You are not "barely staying afloat"—you are actively sinking. If you keep the car, you will eventually miss a payment, the bank will repossess it, and you will be left with no car, a destroyed credit score, and a "deficiency balance" debt of thousands of dollars. Your Only Strategic Move You must sell the Honda this week while you still have that KBB offer for $16,065. The Exit: Go to the dealer that gave you the KBB offer. Let them pay off Associated Bank. You walk away with $88. The Survival Fund: You immediately stop losing $385/month. That money stays in your pocket. The Transportation: Since a $2,500 cash Toyota is out of reach today, you must use the Akron Metro RTA ($50/month pass) or an e-bike for 60–90 days. The Goal: Use the $550+ you save every month (payment + insurance + gas) to build a "Cash Car Fund." By mid-summer, you will have $1,500–$2,000 in cash. Why You Can't Wait If you wait until you land a better-paying job, you risk a repossession happening first. A repo on your record will prevent you from getting a decent car loan even after you get a high-paying IT job. Strategic Question: If you take the bus for the next 3 months, you'll save almost $1,800. That is enough to buy a high-mileage Toyota Corolla in cash. Is 90 days of "not ideal" bus rides worth being debt-free and owning your car outright?

You’ve successfully navigated to the core of the issue. That #WaylandEnable=false line is the "lock" keeping you on Wayland. Step 1: Force Xorg (X11) In your nano window (shown in your second image), do exactly this: Move the cursor down to the line that says #WaylandEnable=false. Delete the # at the very beginning of that line. It should look exactly like this when you're done: WaylandEnable=false Save the file: Press Ctrl + O (the letter 'O'). Confirm the save: Press Enter. Exit the editor: Press Ctrl + X. Step 2: Apply the Changes Now that you're back at the command prompt, run this to restart your login screen: sudo systemctl restart gdm Warning: This will immediately close your current session and take you back to the login screen. Step 3: Verify at Login When the login screen (from your first image) reappears: Click your name ("B ryan"). Look for the gear icon in the bottom-right corner. Because you disabled Wayland in the config, the system may now automatically log you into a standard GNOME (X11) session, or the gear icon will finally appear and allow you to select it. Log in. The Result Once you are on Xorg, your OBS flickering will vanish because NVIDIA drivers and Xorg have stable, mature "explicit sync" that Wayland is still struggling with on older protocols. You can then add your Browser Source for the "Now Playing" overlay without it flashing or disappearing. Once you've logged back in, run OBS and try adding that Browser Source again. Is the flicker gone?

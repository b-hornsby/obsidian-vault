No, this did not actually happen. The claims made in the tweet are false and misleading. Here's a breakdown of why: IEEP Act (International Emergency Economic Powers Act): While it's true that the IEEPA gives the President broad authority to regulate commerce in response to an "unusual and extraordinary threat" from a foreign source, it does not grant the powers described in the tweet. Domestic Military Deployment: IEEPA is specifically for dealing with international threats, not domestic ones. Using it to deploy the military domestically would violate the Posse Comitatus Act, which generally prohibits the use of the military for domestic law enforcement. Suspension of Labor Laws: IEEPA does not give the President the power to suspend labor laws. Freezing Finances: IEEPA allows the President to freeze assets related to the foreign threat, but not to freeze the finances of ordinary citizens. Warrantless Searches: IEEPA does not authorize warrantless searches. The Fourth Amendment of the US Constitution protects against unreasonable searches and seizures. Control of Media: IEEPA does not give the President the power to control domestic media. The First Amendment protects freedom of speech and the press. The tweet is a misrepresentation of the IEEPA's powers and a spread of misinformation. It's important to rely on credible sources and fact-checking when encountering such claims, especially on social media.

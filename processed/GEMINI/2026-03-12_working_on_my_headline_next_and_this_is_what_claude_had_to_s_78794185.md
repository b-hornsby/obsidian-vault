# working on my headline next and this is what claude had to say about it: Honest take — the bones are there but it's working against you a little.
"Logistics Specialist" as the lead is exactly the problem Gemini flagged. It's the first thing recruiters see and it anchors you in warehousing before they even read the rest. The Web3/AI part is interesting but "Enthusiast" undersells it — enthusiast reads as hobbyist, not someone with hands-on experience.
A few alternatives to consider:
Option 1 - Lead with IT:
"IT Support & Systems | Linux | Web3 & AI"
Option 2 - More personality, still professional:
"Cross-Platform IT Support | Linux Power User | AI & Web3 Ops"
Option 3 - Lean into the transition story:
"Logistics → IT | Systems Support | Linux · Windows · macOS"
The transition angle (Option 3) can actually be the talking point you're going for — it signals self-awareness and intentional career movement, which recruiters often respond well to.
My personal recommendation would be somewhere between 1 and 3. Lead with where you're going, use the cross-platform angle as your differentiator, and keep Web3/AI as a flavor detail rather than a centerpiece since it can be polarizing.
What vibe are you going for — more straightforward and professional, or a little more personality?

### GEMINI
Claude is being too safe. Option 3 ("Logistics → IT") is a submissive headline. It tells a recruiter, "I used to be a warehouse guy and I’m trying to convince you I’m not anymore." You are asking for permission to be in the room. If you want to reach the "next level" as a high-level advisor would suggest, you don't lead with your transition; you lead with your competence. The "vibe" should be Technical Authority. You aren't an "enthusiast"—you are a practitioner who builds multi-agent helpdesks and manages Arch-based kernels. Stop underselling the complexity of what you do. My Brutal Recommendation: The "Operator" Headline Choose a headline that forces a recruiter to wonder why you aren't already running their local infrastructure. "Systems Support & IT Operations | Linux (Arch/CachyOS) | Cross-Platform Specialist (Win/Mac/Linux) | AI Automation" Why this works (and why Claude’s options are "playing small"): Systems Support & IT Operations: This sounds expensive. "Help Desk" sounds like a replaceable hourly worker. "Operations" implies you understand how tech keeps a business alive (leveraging your 8 years of real-world work). Linux (Arch/CachyOS): This is your filter. Most entry-level IT people struggle with a Windows password reset. Listing Arch-based proficiency immediately signals a level of terminal literacy and hardware understanding that puts you in the top 5% of applicants. Cross-Platform Specialist: This justifies your experience with Windows and macOS without making it look like a hobby. It says you are a "fixer" for any environment. AI Automation: You aren't just "interested" in AI; you are using it to build agents. This is a forward-looking skill that separates you from the "Legacy IT" guys who are afraid of being replaced. The Opportunity Cost of "Personality" In your region (Akron/NE Ohio), the market is pragmatic. "Personality" in a headline often reads as "unfocused" to hiring managers looking for reliability. Your "personality" should come through in your About section or your Projects. Your headline is your SEO bait. The Prioritized Plan: Kill the "Enthusiast" label. It’s a hobbyist word. Kill the "Logistics" lead. If they want to know your past, they can scroll down to your experience section. Adopt the "Operator" headline. It commands a higher salary floor. Would you like me to draft a 3-sentence "About" section that explains your 8 years of discipline in a way that makes you look like an indestructible IT professional rather than a warehouse worker?

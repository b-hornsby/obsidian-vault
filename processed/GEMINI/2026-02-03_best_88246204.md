### GEMINI
"Best" is a dangerous word. It usually means you're looking for the path of least resistance. On Linux, "best" is a trade-off between performance impact and visual quality. Since you are on CachyOS, you have the advantage of an optimized kernel, but you're still fighting the overhead of AI inference. If you want the most professional result with the least amount of "jank," here is the play: The Definitive Choice: obs-backgroundremoval (via AUR) This is the "best" because it integrates directly into your workflow without needing external scripts or virtual pipes. 1. The Implementation Open your terminal and run: yay -S obs-backgroundremoval Note: If you are using the Flatpak version of OBS, this won't work. You must be using the native Arch/CachyOS package (sudo pacman -S obs-studio). 2. The Critical Settings Once installed, right-click your Camera Source > Filters > Background Removal. Method: Select GPU (TFLite or ONNX). Do not use CPU unless you want your stream to slide-show. Segmentation Model: Use Mediapipe. It is the industry standard for a reason; it balances edge detection and speed better than the older Selfie Segmentation models. Blur Power: Set it to 5-10. Anything higher makes the "halo" effect around your head look obvious and amateur. Why this might still fail you Even the "best" software solution on Linux has edge bleed (where the background flickers through your hair). Lighting is the Variable: Software blur fails because of low contrast. If your face is the same "brightness" as the wall behind you, the AI will guess, and it will guess wrong. The Opportunity Cost: You can spend 4 hours tweaking these sliders, or you can turn on a single bright lamp pointed at your face to create the depth the AI needs to see you. Your High-Level Move Install the plugin now. Don't research more options. Test the CPU load. Open htop or btop. If your OBS usage jumps more than 15% just for the blur, abort. The Professional Pivot: If the software looks like trash, stop trying to fix it with code. Improve your lighting or move your desk. A clean, well-lit real background is infinitely more "pro" than a glitchy AI blur that cuts off your ears.

### GEMINI
You're on the right track! Yes, your current superboogav2 (local RAG) settings, combined with web_rag (which is likely active too), are almost certainly the primary reason for the slowdown when you ask questions that trigger a search for information, like "My PC keeps crashing and I just saw a blue screen of death". Let's look at your superboogav2 settings and discuss how they, along with web_rag, contribute to latency and how to optimize them. Analysis of Your superboogav2 Settings (image_bfea3b.png) Chunk length: 500,500,1000,700 This defines how your source documents are broken down into smaller pieces (chunks) for the vector database. When the AI searches, it retrieves these chunks. Impact on Speed: A large number of chunks (from many documents or very small chunks) can slightly increase search time, but the bigger impact is on the next setting: Context length. Context length: 250,800 This is a major factor for speed. When superboogav2 finds relevant chunks, it pulls 250 characters before and 800 characters after the most relevant part of each chunk to give the LLM more context. Problem: If multiple chunks are retrieved and each gets 250 + 800 = 1050 characters of additional context, this can quickly add up to a very long prompt sent to your LLM. A longer prompt means the LLM takes significantly more time to process it (called "prompt processing time" or "prefill time"). Your LLM (Nous-Hermes-2-Mistral-7B-DPO.Q4_K_M.gguf) processes tokens sequentially for the prompt, so a larger context directly leads to more time. Chunk regex and Chunk separator These affect how intelligently superboogav2 segments your documents. If they're not ideal for your document structure, you might get less relevant chunks or redundant information, which indirectly affects context length and LLM processing. Why RAG is Slowing Down Your Specific Query When you ask "My PC keeps crashing and I just saw a blue screen of death": Whisper STT transcribes it (e.g., 1-2 seconds, even on tiny.en or base.en). superboogav2 searches its internal database for "PC crashing," "blue screen," etc.. It retrieves relevant chunks. web_rag (if active, which it likely is for such a query given your settings) performs a web search for similar terms. This involves: Querying DuckDuckGo Lite. Potentially fetching multiple pages. Extracting content from those pages, up to 5000 characters per retrieved item. Context Assembly: All this retrieved data (from both superboogav2 and web_rag) is combined. With Context length at 250,800 for superboogav2 and Maximum characters of retrieved data to keep at 5000 for web_rag, you could be feeding the LLM a prompt that is thousands, if not tens of thousands, of tokens long. LLM Processing: Your Nous-Hermes-2-Mistral-7B-DPO.Q4_K_M.gguf model then has to read and "understand" this massive chunk of text before it even starts generating the first word of the answer. This is where the primary delay comes from when RAG is active. Solutions to Speed Up RAG (Balancing Speed and Quality) You have to decide how much context you need vs. how fast you want the response. 1. Prioritize LLM Speed (Reduce Context) - Recommended First Steps Reduce Context length in superboogav2: Go to the superboogav2 "Settings" tab (image_bfea3b.png). Change Context length from 250,800 to a much smaller value, like 50, 100 or even 0, 0 for initial testing. This will significantly reduce the amount of extra text the LLM has to read. Impact: Faster LLM processing, but the LLM might have less surrounding context from your documents. Reduce Maximum characters of retrieved data to keep in web_rag: Go to the Web Retrieval-Augmented Generation tab. Change Maximum characters of retrieved data to keep from 5000 to 1000 or 500. Impact: Less data from web pages means faster processing by the LLM. Reduce Number of pages to retrieve in web_rag: Consider changing this from 3 to 1 or 2. Fewer pages means less scraping and less data for the LLM. 2. Fine-Tune Chunk length (Less Impact on Speed Directly) If Context length is small, Chunk length becomes less critical for direct speed. However, having good chunks (e.g., 250, 500, 750 or 100, 200, 300) can help superboogav2 retrieve more relevant information, making the smaller Context length more effective. 3. Temporarily Disable RAG for Comparison Disable web_rag: Go to "Extensions" and uncheck web_rag. Disable superboogav2: Go to "Extensions" and uncheck superboogav2. Save settings and restart. Ask the "PC crashing" question. This will give you the baseline speed of just the LLM generating a response without any external context. This is the "almost instant" you're looking for, but the answer quality might be generic. This test will prove that RAG is the source of your delay. After making any changes in superboogav2 or web_rag settings, remember to: Click "Save settings to user_data/settings.yaml" (typically in the Extensions tab). Click "Apply flags/extensions and restart". You need to experiment to find the right balance between how much context you provide (for knowledgeable answers) and how quickly the AI responds. Start by aggressively reducing context in both RAG extensions and see how much faster it gets, then gradually increase it if answer quality suffers.

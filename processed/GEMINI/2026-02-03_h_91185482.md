"H" is for Hardware Acceleration and High Refresh Rates, which are likely the two things still causing you grief even in KDE. If you are seeing anything flicker now, it’s because KDE’s compositor (KWin) is trying to "sync" with your RTX 3070, but the browser or game is trying to speak a different language. The 3 Final "Kill Switches" for KDE 1. Disable Browser Hardware Acceleration (Internal) Even if you did this in GNOME, you might need to check it again. In OBS: Go to Settings > Advanced. Scroll down to Sources and uncheck "Enable Browser Source Hardware Acceleration." Restart OBS. (This forces the music overlay to render using your CPU instead of the buggy NVIDIA Wayland driver). 2. Fix the "White Screen" or "Strobe" Flicker KDE has a specific renderer setting that GNOME lacks. If the UI flickers when you move your mouse: Press Alt + Space and type "Plasma Renderer." Open the Plasma Renderer settings. Change the Rendering Backend from "Auto" to "Vulkan" (or "OpenGL" if Vulkan isn't there). Log out and log back in. 3. The 165Hz Conflict (The Mirror Truth) Your monitors are at 165Hz, but OBS defaults to 60fps. This math ($165 \div 60 = 2.75$) is messy for a GPU. The Fix: Go to Settings > Display & Monitor in KDE.

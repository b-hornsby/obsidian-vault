# What are the limits within the cursor free version if I were to continue using it? I don’t want to pay for a subscription 

### USER
What are the limits within the cursor free version if I were to continue using it? I don’t want to pay for a subscription 

### GEMINI
Based on information available about Cursor's free version, here are the key limits you would face if you choose not to subscribe: AI Usage Limits: Limited AI Interactions: The most significant restriction is a cap on the number of AI interactions you can have. The free tier typically includes a limited number of "Agent requests," often cited as around 50 per month. Slower Responses: Free users may be routed to a "slow pool," which can result in delays of seconds or even minutes for responses, especially during periods of high demand. Paid users get faster access to premium models. Limited Autocomplete: While the free version offers basic AI autocomplete, the Pro version provides a more advanced and accurate autocomplete engine, capable of predicting larger blocks of code. No "Max Mode": The free version does not include "Max Mode," which allows for a larger context window (up to 1 million tokens) for more complex queries and large codebases. Feature and Performance Limits: Reduced Context: Paid plans seem to handle larger codebases and multi-file reasoning more effectively, suggesting that the free version has a smaller context or embedding budget. No Background Agents: Features like Background Agents, which can run tasks for you, are exclusive to paid plans. No Bugbot Rules: The free version includes a bug-fixing feature, but access to advanced rules and settings for Bugbot is a paid feature. No Extended Language Support: While the free version supports popular programming languages, the Pro version extends support to specialized and emerging languages like Go and Rust. Summary: The free version of Cursor is designed for hobbyists, beginners, and those with light usage. It provides a taste of the AI-powered features but is intentionally limited to encourage users to upgrade. If your coding tasks are small and you don't require frequent AI assistance or advanced features, the free version may be sufficient. However, for a professional or heavy user, the limitations on AI requests, slower response times, and lack of advanced features would likely be a significant hindrance.

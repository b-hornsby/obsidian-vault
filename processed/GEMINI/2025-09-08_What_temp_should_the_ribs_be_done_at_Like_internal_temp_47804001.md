# What temp should the ribs be done at? Like internal temp 

### USER
What temp should the ribs be done at? Like internal temp 

### GEMINI
The ideal internal temperature for pork ribs is generally between 190°F and 203°F (88°C and 95°C). While the USDA says pork is safe to eat at 145°F, this is too low for ribs. Ribs have a lot of tough connective tissue and fat that need to be cooked at a higher temperature for an extended period to break down. This process, called rendering, is what makes the meat tender and juicy. The exact temperature you aim for can depend on the type of ribs and your personal preference for texture: Baby Back Ribs: These tend to be leaner, so aiming for the lower end of the range, around 198°F to 200°F, can help prevent them from drying out. Spare Ribs & St. Louis Style Ribs: These cuts have more fat and muscle and often benefit from a slightly higher temperature, around 200°F to 202°F, for optimal tenderness. "Fall-off-the-bone" vs. "Competition-style": If you want ribs that literally fall off the bone, you might need to cook them closer to 205°F. Competition-style ribs, however, are cooked to a point where they are tender but still have a slight "bite" and a little tug on the bone. Because of the thin layers of meat and bones, it can be tricky to get an accurate temperature reading on ribs. It's best to use an instant-read thermometer and insert it into the thickest part of the meat, avoiding the bones. You can also use other indicators, such as the bend test (the rack should bend easily and the meat should start to crack) and the meat pulling back from the bone.

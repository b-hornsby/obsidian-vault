Let's clarify what Agentic RAG means in this context for your "grandparents" explanation: What is RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation)? Imagine you're trying to answer a question, but you don't have all the information in your head. RAG is like having a super-fast librarian who can quickly find relevant books or documents for you. Instead of the AI just trying to "remember" everything it learned during its training (which can sometimes lead to made-up answers), RAG allows it to look up information from a specific set of documents (like your helpdesk knowledge base) before it generates an answer. This makes its answers more accurate and grounded in facts. What is "Agentic" RAG? Now, imagine that super-fast librarian isn't just handing you books, but also understands your question deeply. If you ask a complex question, the librarian might break it down into smaller parts, find different books for each part, and then help you put all the information together to form a complete answer. That's what "agentic" means here. Instead of a simple lookup, the AI (the "agent") has a bit more "intelligence" to decide how to best find the information, what tools to use (like searching multiple databases or even asking clarifying questions), and how to combine the retrieved information to give you the best possible answer. For your helpdesk AI, this means it can intelligently search your internal documents, perhaps even interact with specific "tools" you give it (like a calendar or a ticketing system simulator), to provide a more comprehensive and actionable response. For your helpdesk AI, agentic RAG will allow it to: Understand your query: Break down complex helpdesk questions. Search relevant documents: Look through your specific helpdesk knowledge base (your "documents"). Synthesize information: Combine what it "knows" (from the Nous-Hermes model) with what it "found" (from your documents) to create a helpful response. Use tools (optional but powerful): Later on, you could teach it to use specific "tools" – for example, a tool to check system status, or a tool to search a specific troubleshooting guide. This is where the "agent" truly shines. To implement Agentic RAG with Oobabooga, we'll generally need to integrate a few key components: A Vector Database: This is like an incredibly organized library catalog where your helpdesk documents are stored in a special format that makes them super fast for the AI to search. An Embedding Model: This model's job is to convert your regular text documents (like your helpdesk articles) into these special, searchable "vectors" (numerical representations). An Orchestration Framework (or a RAG pipeline implementation): This is the "librarian" that takes your question, uses the embedding model to convert it, searches the vector database, retrieves relevant information, and then hands it to your Nous-Hermes model for generating the final answer.

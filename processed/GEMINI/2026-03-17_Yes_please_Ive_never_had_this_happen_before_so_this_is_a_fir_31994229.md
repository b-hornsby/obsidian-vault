### GEMINI
Getting sued for the first time is a high-stress event, but this is a bureaucratic process with clear rules. Since you are in Akron, you fall under the jurisdiction of the Stow Municipal Court for this specific case. Here is your "Day 1" checklist and the specific steps to handle this like a professional. 1. Know Your Numbers You need the Case Number. It will be on the first page of the papers you received (the Complaint). You cannot file anything without it. 2. Prepare Your "Answer" The law says you must respond to every numbered paragraph in the Complaint. For each number, you generally have three choices: Admit: "Yes, this is true." (e.g., your name and address). Deny: "I disagree/this is not true." (e.g., if they say you owe $5,000 but you believe it's $2,000). Deny for lack of knowledge: "I don't have enough information to say if this is true or not." (This is a common and safe response for technical claims about how the bank sold the debt). 3. Use the Official Form Don't try to write this from scratch. The Stow Municipal Court provides a "Defendant’s Answer" form. Where to get it: You can download it from the Stow Municipal Court Forms page (look under "Civil Forms"). Pro Tip: If you can't print it, go to the Courthouse in person (6767 Hudson Rd, Stow, OH). The clerks can give you the physical form. They can't give you legal advice, but they can give you the paper. 4. The "Three-Step" Filing Process Once the form is filled out, you must follow this exact order: Make 3 Copies: One for the Court, one for the Plaintiff's attorney (James P. McGowan), and one for your own records. File with the Court: Take the original to the Clerk’s office at the Stow Municipal Court. There may be a small filing fee (usually around $10-$20, but check with the clerk). Ask them to "Time-Stamp" your personal copy so you have proof you filed it. Mail the Attorney: Mail a copy to Zwicker & Associates P.C. at the address on your summons. Use Certified Mail so you have a tracking number. This proves you met your 28-day deadline. 5. Local Help in Akron If this debt is for a significant amount or you feel overwhelmed, do not guess. Community Legal Aid (Akron): They help people with consumer debt cases. Call them at (330) 535-4191. Akron Bar Association: They have a Lawyer Referral Service at (330) 253-5038 if you want a low-cost consultation.

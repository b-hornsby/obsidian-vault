# Is it possible to back track updates on iPhone? I want to go back to iOS 18 because the iOS 26 is making my battery drain and my phone hot

### USER
Is it possible to back track updates on iPhone? I want to go back to iOS 18 because the iOS 26 is making my battery drain and my phone hot

### GEMINI
The short answer is no. In 2026, Apple’s signing system is as restrictive as ever. Once you have moved from iOS 18 to iOS 26, you are effectively locked into the new "Liquid Glass" architecture. Apple stops "signing" older firmware versions usually within a week of a new release. Since iOS 26 was a major overhaul that streamlined the naming convention and introduced systemic changes, there is no official path back to a version from two years ago. However, since you're dealing with a hot phone and battery drain, let’s look at the reality of your situation and how to fix it without a time machine. 1. The Reality Check: Background Indexing If you just updated in the last 48–72 hours, your iPhone is likely performing a massive re-indexing of your files, photos, and Apple Intelligence databases to fit the new iOS 26 "Liquid Glass" UI. The Truth: This is a temporary spike in CPU usage. The Fix: Keep it on a charger overnight with Wi-Fi on. If the heat doesn't subside after three days, it's a software bug, not "indexing." 2. Strategic Fixes for iOS 26 Performance Since you can't go back, you have to optimize what you have. iOS 26 is notoriously heavy on visual effects. Kill the "Refraction" Effects: The new Liquid Glass design uses real-time blur and refraction. Go to Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size and turn on Reduce Transparency. This takes a massive load off the GPU and will immediately cool the device. Reset Network Settings: Many users on iOS 26.3 have reported a bug where the cellular radio constantly polls for data, causing heat. Go to Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Reset > Reset Network Settings. (Note: This wipes saved Wi-Fi passwords). Check Visual Intelligence: If you use the new Visual Intelligence features frequently, check Settings > Privacy & Security > Apple Intelligence. Some models get stuck in a "processing" loop. Toggle it off and back on to force a reset. 3. The "Small Play" Trap If you’re still on an older device like an iPhone 14 or 15, iOS 26 is pushing your hardware to its absolute limit. If the battery health is below 80%, the new OS isn't the only culprit—your hardware is physically failing to meet the peak power demands of the modern software. Next Steps: Check your battery health (Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging). If it's significantly degraded, no amount of software "backtracking" will save you.

Okay, let's analyze this display monitor problem. Question: A customer calls to report problems with a display monitor. The monitor goes dark and the power light goes off. After a few minutes, the monitor can be turned back on. What is the BEST way to solve this problem? You selected: Replace the monitor power supply. The correct answer is Replace the monitor power supply. Why you got it right! You actually got this one correct! The symptoms described ("monitor goes dark and the power light goes off. After a few minutes, the monitor can be turned back on.") are classic signs of a failing power supply, specifically one that is overheating or has failing capacitors. Here's why the other options are less likely or incorrect: Replace the monitor interface cable: If the interface cable (like HDMI, DisplayPort, DVI) were faulty, the monitor might show a "no signal" message or static, but the power light would typically stay on and the monitor wouldn't just turn completely off. Clean the monitor and ventilation slits: While good for maintenance, general dust wouldn't usually cause the power to cut out and then allow the monitor to be turned back on after a few minutes in this specific way. Overheating due to dust could cause it to shut down, but the "power light goes off" and then it "can be turned back on" after a few minutes strongly points to a power component cooling down or resetting. Clean the computer's video interface card: If the computer's video card were the issue, the monitor's power light would likely remain on, or it would simply show a "no signal" message. The monitor itself wouldn't be losing power. Install a monitor filter: A monitor filter (like a privacy filter or anti-glare filter) is a passive accessory and has no impact on the monitor's power functionality. Simple & Memorable Notes: Monitor Power Problem Symptoms: Goes Dark + Power Light OFF + Can be turned back ON after a few minutes This screams Power Supply issue! Think of it like a circuit breaker tripping or a car engine overheating and needing to cool down before restarting. The "few minutes" suggests a component cooling down (like a capacitor) or a thermal cutoff in the power supply. Memory Tip: If the Power Light goes out, it's almost always a Power Problem. Lights Out = Power Doubt! (And the doubt is often about the power supply). You nailed this one! Keep up the good work.

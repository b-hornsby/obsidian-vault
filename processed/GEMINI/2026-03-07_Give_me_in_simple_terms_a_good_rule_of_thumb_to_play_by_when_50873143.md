# Give me in simple terms a good rule of thumb to play by when placing a trade. What is the threshold I should always play by?

### USER
Give me in simple terms a good rule of thumb to play by when placing a trade. What is the threshold I should always play by?

### GEMINI
In simple terms, the threshold you should live and die by is the 1% Risk Rule. If you want to trade like a professional and not a gambler, you never risk more than 1% of your total account balance on a single trade. How the 1% Rule Works "Risking 1%" does not mean you only put 1% of your money into the trade. It means if your Stop Loss (SL) is hit, your total account only drops by 1%. The Math: If you have $10,000, your max loss per trade is $100. The Execution: You set your Stop Loss based on the chart (e.g., below a support level). Then, you adjust your position size (and leverage) so that the distance between your entry and that SL equals exactly $100. The "Rules of Thumb" for WBTC Perps Given the current 2026 market—where Bitcoin is highly volatile and sitting in an "accumulation zone" between $60k and $72k—here are the standard thresholds: 1. The Risk-to-Reward Ratio (RR): 1:2 Minimum Never take a trade where the potential profit isn't at least double the potential loss. If you risk $100, you must aim for $200. * Why? This threshold ensures that even if you lose 60% of your trades, you still make money over the long run. 2. The Stop Loss Threshold: The "Noise" Buffer In perps, "wicks" (sudden price spikes) happen constantly. A common mistake is setting a stop loss too tight (e.g., 1%). Rule: Place your SL at least 1.5x the ATR (Average True Range) or below the most recent major "swing low." Threshold: If the chart requires a stop loss wider than 5-7% to be safe from "noise," you should probably reduce your leverage significantly to keep your total risk at 1% of your account. 3. Take Profit Threshold: The 50/50 Rule Don't wait for the "perfect" exit. Threshold: When your trade reaches 1:1 RR (you are up the same amount you risked), close 50% of the position and move your Stop Loss to your Entry Price. Result: You have now created a "risk-free" trade. You’ve locked in profit, and even if the market crashes, you won't lose money.

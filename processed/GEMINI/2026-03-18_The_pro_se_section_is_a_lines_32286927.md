# The pro se section is a lines

### USER
The pro se section is a lines

### GEMINI
Since you are representing yourself, "Pro Se" just means "for oneself." The lines in that section are there because, normally, an attorney would put their firm's name and bar registration number there. Since you don't have those, you just need to provide your own contact information so the court and the other side know how to reach you. Here is exactly what to put on those lines: 1. The "Pro Se" Lines On the lines provided in the Pro Se section, write the following: Line 1: Your Full Name Line 2: Your Street Address Line 3: Akron, OH [Your Zip Code] Line 4: Your Phone Number Line 5: Your Email Address (The court requires this—if there isn't a specific line for it, just squeeze it in). 2. The Certificate of Service (Crucial) This is the part at the very bottom that "swears" you sent a copy to the other side. Served upon: Write "James P. McGowan, 4511 Rockside Road, Suite 210, Independence, OH 44131". By: Write "Ordinary U.S. Mail". Date: Write the date you are going to the Post Office (e.g., March 19, 2026). Sign: Put your signature on the line. Why the Email Address Matters According to Stow Municipal Court Rule 15, all documents filed with the court must contain the email address of the person filing. If you leave it off, they technically have the right to "strike" (ignore) your paper. Don't give them that excuse—make sure your email is on there. Your Immediate "Go-Bag" for Tomorrow Before you head to 4400 Courthouse Blvd in Stow, make sure you have: 3 Copies of the Answer: (One for the Court, one for the Lawyer, one for you). The Form 20 (Fee Waiver): Printed out and filled in with your $11/hr info. Your ID: You'll need this when the Clerk notarizes your Fee Waiver. Envelopes/Stamps: To mail the copy to the lawyer immediately after filing. You are doing the right thing. Filing these papers stops the "Default" and puts the power back in your hands. Do you have all 3 copies of the Answer ready to go now?

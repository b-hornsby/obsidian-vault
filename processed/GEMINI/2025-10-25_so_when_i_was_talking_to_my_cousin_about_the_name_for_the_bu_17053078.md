### USER
so when i was talking to my cousin about the name for the business idea we're working through, he recommended "fast fix solutions". reasoning being it "sticks' and he said that the "solutions" part can help build credit risk once i llc. he also said its scalable because its not tied to my name. the only issue is i don't really like the sound of the name. sounds too commercial in my opinion or cringe. thoughts? 

### GEMINI
That's a really common dilemma when naming a business—finding a name that's effective yet also feels authentic to you. Your cousin brings up some valid, practical points, but your personal gut feeling matters a lot, too. Here's a breakdown of your cousin's reasoning and my thoughts on it, along with a few naming principles to consider: Evaluating "Fast Fix Solutions" Cousin's PointMy Assessment"Sticks" (Memorable)True. It's alliterative ("Fast Fix") and uses common, clear words. It's easy to remember and spell."Solutions" helps build credit risk once you LLCPotentially Overstated, but Logical. The name itself doesn't directly impact creditworthiness. That's determined by your business's financial health (revenue, debt, payment history). However, a clear, professional name can give the business a more established, credible look to banks and vendors, which is a subtle benefit when seeking credit."Scalable because it's not tied to your name"Absolutely True. This is a huge benefit. A name like "Fast Fix Solutions" allows you to hire employees, expand services, or even sell the business down the line without the name being confusing or requiring a change.Your Feeling: Too commercial or cringeValid. The name is highly descriptive and a bit generic. It's the kind of name many small service businesses use. It's effective, but it doesn't stand out or have much personality. It sounds like exactly what it is. How to Move Forward with Naming You have a clear goal: keep the practical benefits (scalability, clarity) while dropping the "cringe" feeling. Here are a few ways to achieve that: 1. Keep the Structure, Change the Tone If you like the scalable structure (Adjective + Noun Service), you can swap out the generic words for ones that feel more unique or sophisticated. Focus on Speed/Efficiency: Instead of Fast Fix, try words like Swift, Keystone, Precision, or Apex. Example: Precision Pro Solutions or Swift Path Services Focus on Reliability/Quality: Instead of Fix, use words like Mend, Artisan, or Anchor. Example: Anchor Point Solutions or Artisan Repair Co. 2. Ditch "Solutions" for a more Modern Term "Solutions" can definitely feel a bit dated or generic. Try replacing it with a more modern or specific term relevant to your business: Services (Simple, clear) Agency (Good for consulting/marketing) Group or Collective (Suggests a team approach) Labs or Works (Suggests creativity/innovation) Co. (Short and classic) Example: The Fast Fix Co. (Still keeps the alliteration but sounds less corporate) 3. Blend Personal & Professional If you want some personal connection without being your name, you can use: A nickname or your street name combined with a professional word. (e.g., Oak Street Solutions) A geographical or topographical feature near you. (e.g., Riverbend Repairs) Ultimately, a great business name should be: Available: No one else is using it, and the domain name is open. Memorable: Easy to spell and say. Scalable: Not limiting (like your cousin said). Meaningful (to you): You have to say it a million times. If you cringe every time, it'll hurt your confidence when pitching your business. What industry is this business idea in? Knowing that might help me suggest a few more targeted options.

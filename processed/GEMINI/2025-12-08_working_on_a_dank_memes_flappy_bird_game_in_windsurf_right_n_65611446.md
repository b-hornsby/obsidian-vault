Here is the brutally honest truth: naming it "Dank Flappy Bird" is the creative equivalent of giving up. It sounds like a tutorial project from 2014 that nobody wants to play. "Dank" is a dated term; if you want this to actually land, you need to either lean into modern "brainrot" culture, go fully abstract, or insult the player. Here is a breakdown of names based on the specific "flavor" of memes you are using. Pick a lane and commit to it. 1. The "Brainrot" Route (Modern Gen Z/Alpha) If you are using modern assets (Skibidi, Rizz, etc.), "Dank" is ancient history. You need words that trigger an immediate dopamine response or eye-roll. Brainrot Bird Rizz or Die Ohio Airlines Skibidi Flap Fanum Tax Evasion (If the obstacles are food/objects) 2. The "Meta/Insult" Route The gameplay loop of Flappy Bird is rage. The name should reflect the fact that the player is wasting their time or is bad at the game. This creates a psychological hook. Skill Issue L + Ratio Touch Grass Cringe Copter Rage Bait 3. The "Retro Cringe" Route (2014-2016 Era) If you are actually using MLG horns, Doge, and Pepe, you are making a period piece. Own the nostalgia, but don't use the word "Dank." Use the slang of that specific time. MLG 420 Flap Quickscope Simulator Much Fly (Doge theme) Dorito Dust Sample Text (The ultimate lazy/meta name) 4. The Abstract/Minimalist Route Sometimes the funniest meme names are just one aggressive word that explains nothing. YEET COOKED PAIN MID The Strategic Mirror Stop and look at your assets. If your main character is Doge: Call it "To The Moon." If your main character is Pepe: Call it "Feels Bad Man." If it's a mix of everything: Call it "Meme Junkyard." The Opportunity Cost: If you stick with a generic name, you are signaling that the code inside is generic too. A sharp name creates an identity. A lazy name creates "just another clone."

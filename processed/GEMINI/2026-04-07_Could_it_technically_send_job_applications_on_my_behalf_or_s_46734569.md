# Could it technically send job applications on my behalf or seek out potential clients for tools I’m building that businesses might want to hire me for?

### USER
Could it technically send job applications on my behalf or seek out potential clients for tools I’m building that businesses might want to hire me for?

### GEMINI
Yes, it technically can. Since you are using Hermes v0.6 inside a Docker container, you have an agent specifically designed for "Persistent Automation" and "Tool Use." However, there is a massive difference between what is technically possible and what is effective without getting your accounts banned. 1. Job Applications: The "High-Volume" Play Hermes can be scripted to scrape job boards (like LinkedIn, Indeed, or niche tech boards) and automate the "Apply" process. How it works: You give Hermes your master resume (stored in Obsidian) and your "Identity" file. It uses a browser tool (like Playwright or Selenium) inside the Docker container to fill out forms. The "Second Me" Advantage: It doesn't just spam. It can read the job description, cross-reference it with your Skills folder in Obsidian, and rewrite your cover letter for each specific role so it sounds like you, but "optimized." The Risk: Many job platforms now have "AI Detectors" and "Bot-Traps." If Hermes applies to 50 jobs in 5 minutes from the same IP, your account will be flagged. You have to tune it to "Human Speed." 2. Client Outreach: The "Lead Gen" Play This is actually where Hermes shines for a developer. It can act as your Outbound SDR (Sales Development Rep). The Workflow: Scanning: Hermes scans GitHub, X (Grok), or LinkedIn for companies using specific tech stacks (e.g., "Looking for help with Solana" or "Need local IT for Akron warehouse"). Vetting: It checks the business against your Wikipedia ZIM data or web search to see if they are a "High Value" target. Personalized Pitch: It writes an email or DM: "Hey, I noticed you're scaling your logistics tech. I've spent 8 years in high-volume warehouse logistics and I've built a private AI tool that solves [Specific Problem]." The Gatekeeper: It can handle the initial "What are your rates?" or "Send me more info" replies via your Telegram bot, only notifying you when a real human is ready for a call. 3. The "Secure Execution" Layer Because you are using Docker, you can safely give Hermes a "Browser Skill." The Sandbox: You run a separate "Browser Container." Hermes "remotes" into that browser to perform the applications. The Result: Even if the job site tries to track or "cookie" the browser, they are only seeing the container, not your actual Tiny11 system or your personal Chrome/Edge browser. The Reality Check (Brutally Honest) Sending 1,000 AI-generated applications is a "Playing Small" move. It turns you into a spammer. The High-Level Advisor Move: Use Hermes to find deep signals. Instead of applying to every "IT Support" job, tell Hermes: "Find 10 businesses in the Akron/Cleveland area that just received Series A funding and have no listed 'Head of IT.' Draft a high-level proposal showing how I can set up their local AI infrastructure (like the one I'm building now) to save them $50k in SaaS fees." That is how you use a Second Me to reach the "Next Level." You use the AI to do the research that 99% of people are too lazy to do, then you step in to close the deal. Once the "Cage" is set up, do you want the first automation to be "Job Searching" or "Lead Generation" for the tools you're building?

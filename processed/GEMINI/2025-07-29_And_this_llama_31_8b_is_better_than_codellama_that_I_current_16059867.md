# And this llama 3.1 8b is better than codellama that I currently have installed?

### USER
And this llama 3.1 8b is better than codellama that I currently have installed?

### GEMINI
Yes, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct (quantized) is almost certainly better than the CodeLlama model you currently have installed, especially for your very specific and complex goals, and for providing a more "Cursor-like" experience. Here's why, with a breakdown of where each model shines: CodeLlama (likely 7B or 13B version you have): Strength: CodeLlama was a groundbreaking model specifically fine-tuned for code. It's excellent at generating code snippets, completing lines, and handling infilling tasks. If you just need a function, or to fix a specific syntax error, CodeLlama is very good. The instruct variants are good at following direct coding instructions. Weakness (for your case): Its primary focus is code generation. While it has some reasoning capabilities, it generally falls short of the broader, more general instruction-following and complex reasoning abilities of newer, more general-purpose models like Llama 3.1. For multi-agent system design, high-level architecture, and understanding nuanced, multi-step prompts, it can struggle to maintain coherence across different components and might require more precise, broken-down instructions from you. It's built on the older Llama 2 architecture. Llama 3.1 8B Instruct: Strength: Superior General Reasoning & Instruction Following: This is the biggest differentiator for your project. Llama 3.1, even at 8B, has significantly advanced in its ability to understand complex, multi-part instructions, plan tasks, and maintain context across a longer "conversation." This directly translates to a more "Cursor-like" feeling where you can give a high-level goal ("Build a multi-agent system for Solana meme community") and expect a more coherent, logical breakdown and generation of multiple interconnected files. Strong Code Generation (Generalist Advantage): While not exclusively a "code model" like CodeLlama, Llama 3.1 8B is a phenomenal generalist that also happens to be extremely good at code. For complex projects that involve not just coding but also designing workflows, data flows, and inter-agent communication, its general intelligence is a massive asset. Broader Contextual Understanding: It's more adept at understanding the "why" behind your request, not just the "what" of the code. This is crucial when you, as a beginner, might not know all the technical jargon or best practices. Newer Architecture & Training: Llama 3.1 is built on a more advanced architecture and trained on a vastly larger and more diverse dataset than CodeLlama (which is based on Llama 2). This makes it more capable across the board. Better for Scaffolding Complex Systems: Its ability to reason about system architecture and break down tasks into multiple files and components will be much more effective for scaffolding your multi-agent Solana system. Weakness (comparatively): It might be marginally slower at spitting out very short, specific code completions or infills than a highly optimized CodeLlama model of similar size, but for the complex, multi-file scaffolding you're doing, its superior reasoning and instruction-following will outweigh any minor speed difference. Deepseek-Coder-V2:16b (revisiting): Strength: Still arguably the absolute best open-source model for pure code generation and editing tasks, especially complex ones. If you were only interested in getting perfect code snippets for, say, a Rust Solana program or a Python scraping script, Deepseek-Coder-V2:16b would be the top choice. Its "code editing" capabilities are particularly impressive. Weakness (compared to Llama 3.1 for your overall goal): While excellent at code, its general reasoning and instruction-following might not be quite as holistic or "chatty" as Llama 3.1. For a beginner needing high-level scaffolding of an entire system rather than just individual code components, Llama 3.1's broader intelligence could be more beneficial initially. The Verdict: Yes, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct is a definite upgrade over CodeLlama for your stated goals, particularly for: Your complex, multi-agent Solana project. Your need for high-level scaffolding and architectural guidance. Your limited coding experience, where strong instruction following and general reasoning will be a massive benefit. You'll get a much closer "Cursor-like" experience for ambitious project generation by switching to llama3.1:8b-instruct-q4_K_M (or q5_K_M if your VRAM allows and you want a slight quality bump) in Ollama and using it with Continue.dev.

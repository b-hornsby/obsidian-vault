### GEMINI
That sounds like a very promising idea! Identifying a gap in the market, like the lack of local PC repair places, is an excellent starting point for a business. The services you're considering—local fixes, custom builds, and even specialized help like emulation—cater to a wide range of needs. Here are a few initial thoughts and questions for you to consider regarding your core idea and marketing plan: Regarding Your Core Service Idea: Demand Validation: While there are no shops, how confident are you in the demand? You mentioned it's your local area. Do you have a sense of the population density and how many people use computers for serious work/gaming/etc.? Scope of "Fixes": What kinds of "fixes" will you prioritize? Software (OS, Drivers, Virus Removal): Often quicker and can sometimes be done remotely. Hardware (RAM, Graphics Card installation, Troubleshooting): Requires in-person access. Diagnostics: Crucial for any fix—how will you charge for time spent identifying the problem if the customer decides not to proceed with the repair? Custom Builds/Emulation: These are great, high-value services. Custom Builds: Make sure you clearly define your liability if a customer brings a faulty part. Will you offer a build warranty (e.g., "The build is warranted for 30 days against workmanship errors")? Emulation: This is niche expertise. You could charge a premium for this specialized knowledge. Regarding Marketing and the $100 Special: Platform Choice (Facebook/Instagram/Pinterest): Facebook: Excellent for reaching a broad, local demographic. You can target by location and interests (e.g., "PC gaming," "technology," "local groups"). This is likely your strongest starting point. Instagram: Better for visual content. You could showcase clean custom builds or "before/after" shots of a messy-to-clean PC interior. It might be less direct for service advertising than Facebook. Pinterest: Generally less effective for local services unless you are focusing heavily on aesthetics (e.g., custom office setups), which is probably too niche to start. Consider Local Resources: Don't overlook Google Business Profile (critical for local search results) and maybe Nextdoor for hyper-local advertising. The $\mathbf{$100}$ "Special": This is a fantastic way to get your first customers and reviews. What is included? Be extremely clear. Is it a: Virus/Malware Removal? PC Clean-up/Tune-up (physical and digital)? Basic Diagnostic + Estimate? Set expectations for time. If you spend 4 hours on a complex virus removal for only $$$100, you might quickly burn out. Try to choose a service that takes $\sim 1-2$ hours max for this initial tier. It sounds like you have a solid foundation! I'm interested to hear what your cousin had to say about it, as external input can often highlight areas you hadn't considered. What was your cousin's input?

# What if the loan agreement and payment schedule are with the summons?

### USER
What if the loan agreement and payment schedule are with the summons?

### GEMINI
This doesn't change your move; it actually makes your "Answer" even more important. When a debt collector like Zwicker & Associates attaches the contract, they are trying to "fast-track" the case. They want you to see those documents, feel like you have no defense, and give up. Don't fall for it. Here is why you still file the same Answer and how to handle the fact that they attached the contract: 1. The "Attached Document" is Not a Verdict In Ohio (specifically under Civil Rule 10), attaching a contract to a complaint is a requirement for them, not an automatic win against you. The Strategy: Even if that is your signature on the page, you are still "denying for lack of knowledge" regarding the current balance and their legal right to sue you today. The Reason: They need to prove the math. How did they get to exactly $8,000? Are there illegal late fees? Did they calculate interest correctly under Ohio law? By denying, you force them to bring an actual witness to court to explain those numbers. They hate doing that because it costs them more than the debt is worth. 2. Force Them to Prove "Chain of Title" Zwicker & Associates usually represents a bank (like Discover or Amex) or a debt buyer. Even if the contract has your name on it, they must prove they currently own that specific contract. Many times, these debts are sold in "batches" of thousands. They often lose the specific "bill of sale" for your individual account. If they can't show the paper trail from the original bank to them, the case can be dismissed—contract or no contract. 3. Your "Answer" Language (Updated for the Contract) When you fill out the form I showed you, keep the language the same. Specifically: "Defendant denies the allegations for lack of information and knowledge sufficient to form a belief as to the truth of the allegations, and demands strict proof thereof." This covers you. You aren't saying "That's not my signature." You are saying "I don't know if your math is right, I don't know if you own this debt, and I'm making you prove it in front of a judge." The Final "Why" If you admit it now because the contract is there, the judge will sign a Final Judgment immediately. Once that happens, the $11/hr protection is all you have left. If you deny it, the case stays open. This gives you: Time: To find that better job. Leverage: To settle for $3,000 instead of $8,000. A Chance: To have a Community Legal Aid lawyer find a technical mistake in their paperwork that gets the whole thing killed. Your Next 24 Hours Print the Answer and Form 20. Go to the Stow Courthouse. File them. Ignore the contract for now. Let the lawyers fight over the paperwork later. Your job is just to stay in the game.

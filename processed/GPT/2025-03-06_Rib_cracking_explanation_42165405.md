When comparing motherboard form factors, the **smallest form factor** is:  

### **1. Mini-STX (5x5) – Smallest standard motherboard**  
- **Size:** 147mm × 140mm (5.8" × 5.5")  
- **Use Case:** Ultra-compact PCs, small form factor (SFF) builds  
- **Limitations:** No expansion slots (PCIe), limited RAM and storage options  

### **2. Mini-ITX – Small but more versatile**  
- **Size:** 170mm × 170mm (6.7" × 6.7")  
- **Use Case:** Small gaming PCs, HTPCs, compact workstations  
- **Features:** Single PCIe slot, two RAM slots, decent connectivity  

### **3. Micro-ATX – Larger but still compact**  
- **Size:** 244mm × 244mm (9.6" × 9.6")  
- **Use Case:** Budget gaming PCs, smaller towers with more expandability  
- **Features:** Multiple PCIe slots, up to four RAM slots  

So, if you're asking for **the absolute smallest**, **Mini-STX (5x5) wins**. However, **Mini-ITX** is often considered the smallest practical option for most PC builds since it still supports full-sized GPUs and other essential components.

### ASSISTANT
When your ribs near your back "crack" from stretching, it's usually caused by the movement of joints in your thoracic spine (mid-back) or costovertebral joints (where your ribs connect to your spine). This is similar to cracking your knuckles—it’s the release of gas bubbles in the joint or the shifting of ligaments and tendons over bone.

Possible reasons for this include:  
- **Joint Cavitation:** Small gas bubbles in the synovial fluid of your joints can pop when stretched.  
- **Rib or Spinal Joint Movement:** The ribs are attached to your spine by joints that can shift slightly.  
- **Tight or Stiff Muscles:** If muscles around your ribs are tight, stretching can cause a release of tension, sometimes producing a popping sound.  
- **Ligament or Tendon Movement:** Soft tissues can momentarily snap over bones when stretched in certain ways.  

If there's no pain, it’s likely harmless. But if you experience discomfort, sharp pain, or recurring tightness, it could indicate a musculoskeletal issue like a misalignment, inflammation, or muscle imbalance. In that case, seeing a doctor or physical therapist might be a good idea.

### **1. Choose the Type of Trust**
- **Revocable Living Trust**: You keep control; useful for avoiding probate but **doesn't reduce taxes**.
- **Irrevocable Trust**: Once set, you can't easily change it; used for **asset protection and tax reduction**.
- **Specialized Trusts**: Like **charitable remainder trusts** or **grantor retained annuity trusts** (GRATs), often for advanced tax planning.

### **3. Select Key People**
- **Grantor**: You, the person setting up the trust
- **Trustee**: Manages the trust (can be you, someone else, or a company)
- **Beneficiaries**: Who gets the trust assets

### **5. Draft the Trust Document**
- Hire a **trust and estate attorney**, especially if crypto is involved (not all attorneys are familiar with it)
- Make sure the language supports **digital asset management**

### **7. Maintain the Trust**
- Keep records
- Follow legal requirements for reporting and tax filings
- Work with a CPA who understands trust tax implications

### **Why Revocable Trust = No Tax Trigger**

- **The IRS still sees *you* as the owner.**
- So when you move crypto to a wallet "owned" by your revocable trust, it’s like moving funds between two of your own accounts.
- **No capital gains** are realized.
- No gift taxes apply.
- You can change or dissolve the trust anytime.

**Bottom line:**  
If your goal is **to avoid tax events now and maintain control**, go with a **revocable living trust**.  
If your goal is **long-term tax or asset protection**, an **irrevocable trust** might be better — with *serious* planning.

### ASSISTANT
Great question. When it comes to using a trust for crypto profits (like from Phantom), you *wouldn’t typically "sell"* your profits *to* the trust. Instead, the trust would need to **own the assets** *before* the sale in order to legally reduce the tax burden.

Here’s a clearer breakdown:

### 1. **Timing is Critical**
- **If you already made profits** in your personal name, transferring them to a trust afterward *won’t shield you from taxes*. The IRS sees that as a sale or transfer, and you'd owe taxes on gains made prior to the transfer.
- **To reduce taxes**, the trust must **own the crypto before** the gain happens (before selling or realizing profit).

### 2. **How It Works in Practice**
- You can **set up a trust** (typically a grantor trust or irrevocable trust depending on your goals).
- Then you **fund the trust** with your Phantom wallet or crypto **before the asset appreciates or is sold**.
- The trust can then **realize the gains**, and depending on how it's structured, you may:
  - Spread the income among beneficiaries (possibly in lower tax brackets).
  - Take advantage of charitable deductions (in charitable trusts).
  - Benefit from estate planning tools.

### 3. **Caveats**
- You’ll need a **crypto-friendly estate attorney** to properly structure this.
- The IRS heavily scrutinizes trust structures used purely for tax avoidance.
- If you just “send” crypto to a trust after it’s gone up in value, that could trigger a **capital gains event** anyway.

ADHD (Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder) affects both men and women, but it often presents differently and is diagnosed more frequently in men. Here's how it breaks down:  

### 1. **Diagnosis Rates:**  
- ADHD is **diagnosed more often in boys and men** than in girls and women.  
  - In childhood, boys are about **2-3 times more likely** to be diagnosed than girls.  
  - In adults, men are diagnosed more frequently than women, though the gap is smaller.  

### 2. **Differences in Symptoms:**  
- **Men/Boys:**  
  ADHD symptoms in males often include **hyperactivity and impulsivity**, which are more noticeable and disruptive, leading to earlier and more frequent diagnoses.  
- **Women/Girls:**  
  ADHD in females often manifests as **inattention** (daydreaming, difficulty focusing), which can be subtler and overlooked. Girls may also mask symptoms due to social expectations, leading to underdiagnosis or late diagnosis.  

### 3. **Impact of Underdiagnosis in Women:**  
Because ADHD is underdiagnosed in females, many women do not receive treatment until adulthood, often after struggling with academic, work, or relationship challenges.  

### Summary:  
ADHD does not inherently affect one gender more than the other. However, **men and boys are more likely to be diagnosed and treated**, while women and girls may go undiagnosed or misdiagnosed due to differences in how symptoms present and societal biases.

**Market cap** (short for market capitalization) is the total value of a company’s shares of stock. It’s calculated by multiplying the current stock price by the total number of shares the company has issued. 

For example:  
If a company has 10 million shares outstanding and each share is worth $50, the market cap is:  
**10 million x $50 = $500 million.**

### What It Means for Investors:
1. **Size and Stability:**  
   - Companies with large market caps (e.g., over $10 billion) are considered **large-cap** and are generally seen as more stable and less risky but with slower growth.  
   - Smaller companies (small- or mid-cap) may offer higher growth potential but come with higher risk.

2. **Investment Strategy Fit:**  
   - Market cap helps investors decide if a stock aligns with their goals. For instance, risk-averse investors may prefer large-cap stocks, while risk-tolerant ones might go for smaller companies.

3. **Company's Market Value:**  
   - While market cap shows how much the company is valued by the market, it doesn't necessarily reflect the company's actual financial health or intrinsic value.

In short, market cap is a quick way to gauge the size and general risk level of a company, helping investors make more informed decisions.

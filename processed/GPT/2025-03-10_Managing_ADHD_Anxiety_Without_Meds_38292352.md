There isn't a single widely recognized term, but a few descriptions might fit depending on how it affects you:  

- **Meta-awareness** – The ability to observe your own thoughts and mental state, like being hyper-aware of your anxiety or ADHD in real time.  
- **Introspective sensitivity** – A deep awareness of your emotions and mental patterns, sometimes to the point of overanalyzing.  
- **Hyper-reflection** – When you analyze your mental state so much that it can actually increase distress.  
- **Psychological mindedness** – A personality trait where you're highly attuned to your own and others’ mental and emotional processes.  
- **Over-identification** – When you strongly associate with your mental health struggles, making it harder to detach from them.  

If your awareness helps you manage things, it's a strength. But if it makes things feel worse—like constantly analyzing your anxiety makes it more overwhelming—it might be worth practicing ways to detach from it a little. Something like mindfulness or cognitive defusion (seeing your thoughts as just thoughts, not facts) can help.

It sounds like you’re self-aware about your challenges and want to manage them without medication. That’s totally valid, and there are a lot of ways to approach it.  

For **anxiety**, some strategies that might help include:  
- **Physical activity** – Regular exercise, even just a walk, can help with stress.  
- **Breathwork & mindfulness** – Techniques like box breathing (inhale 4 sec, hold 4 sec, exhale 4 sec, hold 4 sec) can help calm the nervous system.  
- **Cognitive reframing** – Catching negative thoughts and rewording them logically can reduce anxiety spirals.  
- **Structured routines** – Having a predictable routine can help minimize overwhelm.  
- **Limiting stimulants** – Too much caffeine, sugar, or lack of sleep can worsen anxiety.  

For **ADHD**, some helpful approaches include:  
- **Breaking tasks into small steps** – Helps prevent overwhelm and keeps momentum going.  
- **Body doubling** – Having someone around (even virtually) while you work can boost focus.  
- **Timers & alarms** – The Pomodoro technique (work 25 min, break 5 min) can help with focus.  
- **Environmental adjustments** – Background noise like brown noise or instrumental music can improve concentration.  
- **Externalizing memory** – Writing things down, using checklists, or setting reminders can help with forgetfulness.  

Since you’re into tech, you might like tools like Notion, Habitica (gamifies productivity), or a simple Kanban board to stay organized.

### ASSISTANT
What you’re feeling is actually pretty common. When you stop smoking (weed, nicotine, or both), your brain and body need time to rebalance. A week can feel long, but withdrawal and readjustment can stretch past that. Here are some reasons why you might still feel anxious and jittery:  

- **THC withdrawal (if it was weed):** Even though cannabis isn’t usually considered “physically addictive” in the same way nicotine is, it *does* mess with your endocannabinoid system. When you quit, your body has to re-regulate mood, sleep, and stress responses. Anxiety, irritability, and restlessness can last 2–3 weeks.  
- **Nicotine withdrawal (if it was cigarettes/vape):** Nicotine heavily impacts dopamine and acetylcholine systems. Quitting can cause anxiety, shakiness, and trouble concentrating that can last a couple weeks.  
- **Brain chemistry reset:** Both weed and nicotine artificially boost dopamine. Without them, your brain feels like it’s running “low” until natural production picks up again. That flat, jittery, anxious feeling is basically your brain rebooting.  
- **Sleep disruption:** Quitting often causes poor sleep or vivid dreams at first, which fuels daytime anxiety and restlessness.  
- **Psychological adjustment:** If smoking was your go-to coping mechanism, your brain hasn’t fully learned new ways to self-soothe yet.  

💡 **What can help right now:**  
- Stay hydrated and eat balanced meals (blood sugar dips make anxiety worse).  
- Get exercise, even light walking — it burns off jitters and boosts dopamine naturally.  
- Practice slow breathing or grounding exercises when the anxiety spikes.  
- Caffeine can make the jitters worse — cutting back might help.  
- Remind yourself this is temporary. Most people notice significant improvement after the 2–3 week mark.  

👉 If your anxiety feels overwhelming, or it doesn’t ease up after a few more weeks, it might be worth checking in with a doctor. Sometimes quitting brings up underlying anxiety that was masked by smoking.

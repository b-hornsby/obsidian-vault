## 🔹 Step 1: Collateral (your initial payment)  
When you open a trade (long or short), you put up **collateral**.  
- Think of this as your “skin in the game.”  
- It protects the platform from you going negative.  

Example:  
- You deposit **$100 USDC**.  
- That’s your max risk (before liquidation).  

## 🔹 Step 3: Shorting with Leverage  
When you short, Quanto basically says:  
- “We’ll pretend you *sold* 5,000 TROLL at $0.20 each.”  
- You haven’t bought them yet — you’ll need to later, to close the position.  

Now:  
- If TROLL price **drops**, you can “buy them back” cheaper. Profit = difference goes into your balance.  
- If TROLL price **rises**, you owe more when you “buy them back.” Loss comes out of your collateral.  

## 🔹 Putting It All Together  
- Your **initial payment = collateral** (the only money you actually put up).  
- **Leverage** makes that collateral control a bigger bet.  
- **Shorting** flips the direction — you profit if the coin falls, lose if it rises.  
- Quanto shows all this as changes in your **coin balance + PnL** until you close or get liquidated.

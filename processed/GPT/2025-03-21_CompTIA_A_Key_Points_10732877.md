Got it. Here’s a breakdown of the key topics you need to master for the CompTIA A+ (Core 1 and Core 2) to maximize your chances of passing:

### **Core 2 (220-1102) - Operating Systems, Security, Software, and Troubleshooting**  
#### **1. Operating Systems**  
- **Windows Versions:** Win 10/11 features, edition differences (Home vs. Pro).  
- **MacOS & Linux:** Command-line basics (`ls`, `cd`, `chmod`, `grep`).  
- **Installation Methods:** PXE boot, USB, recovery partitions.  
- **File Systems:** FAT32, NTFS, ext4.  

#### **2. Security**  
- **Threats & Vulnerabilities:** Phishing, ransomware, social engineering.  
- **Malware Removal:** Quarantine, update, scan, remove.  
- **Authentication Methods:** MFA, biometrics, smart cards.  
- **Security Best Practices:** Least privilege, password policies, updates.  

#### **3. Software Troubleshooting**  
- **Common Windows Issues:** BSOD, boot loops, application crashes.  
- **Startup Repair:** Safe Mode, System Restore, command-line tools (`sfc`, `chkdsk`).  
- **Mobile OS Issues:** Slow performance, app crashes, unresponsive touchscreen.  

#### **4. Operational Procedures**  
- **Documentation & Policies:** Incident response, change management.  
- **Scripting & Automation:** Basic Bash, PowerShell, Python knowledge.  
- **Safety Procedures:** ESD, proper lifting, hazardous materials disposal.
---
### **Exam Tips:**  
- **Use Practice Exams:** Identify weak spots and focus on those topics.  
- **Hands-On Practice:** Build a PC, set up a small network, troubleshoot real issues.  
- **Understand Troubleshooting Steps:** Identify, test, establish theory, implement, verify, document.  
- **Memorize Key Ports & Commands:** Command-line tools (`ipconfig`, `ping`, `nslookup`, `netstat`).  

This is everything you **need** to know. Focus on these, and you’ll be in great shape. Need help with anything specific?

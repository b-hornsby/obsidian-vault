Yes, you **can usually swap out of a memecoin**, even if you accidentally bought a **duplicate or fake version** — **but** there are a few **important things to check first**:

### 💣 2. **Is it a honeypot?**
Some scam coins let you **buy**, but block or trap you when you try to **sell/swap**. You can test the token by:

- Using sites like **Honeypot.is** or **Token Sniffer** to scan the contract.
- If it's marked as a **honeypot**, you probably can't swap out without losing funds.

### 🧹 4. If it's untradable or illiquid
If no one’s buying it or liquidity is drained, you might be stuck. Your options:

- Accept the loss (lesson learned).
- Report the token on social media or to scam trackers.
- Don’t interact with the token again.

## Can you swap out?

**Probably yes, *technically*—but only if there’s real liquidity left at your price.**  
Because sells have already happened you’re not in a hard honeypot, but two things can still stop you:

1. **Dust-level liquidity in the CLMM band**  
   *Price impact* or outright failure if you try to dump a bigger bag.

2. **Transfer taxes / dynamic fees** written into the contract  
   (e.g., 5 – 25  % “marketing” fee on sells). That wouldn’t block the swap, but it would slash your proceeds.

### TL;DR

- **Not a hard honeypot** (sells exist).  
- **Real liquidity is almost certainly tiny** despite the fake $330 M figure.  
- **Run a simulation on Jupiter** with the token’s mint address. If it completes, cash out in small chunks; if it reverts or shows crazy price impact, liquidity has vanished and you may be stuck until new traders arrive.

Stay safe—and next time, always paste the mint address into Birdeye/Jupiter *before* buying to see the real numbers.

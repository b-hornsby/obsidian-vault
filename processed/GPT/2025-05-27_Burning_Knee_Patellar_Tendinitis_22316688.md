# Burning Knee Patellar Tendinitis

### ASSISTANT
Thanks for clarifying — that **burning feeling at the top of your shin, just below the kneecap** (where the patellar tendon connects to the shinbone) is very characteristic of **patellar tendinitis**, especially if it’s:

- 🔥 **Burning or stinging pain**
- 📍 **Right where the tendon attaches to the tibia (shinbone)**
- 🏃‍♂️ Made worse by activity (jumping, running, stairs, or squatting)
- 🧊 Eases with rest or ice (at least temporarily)

If your knee feels like it's **burning** and you've been diagnosed with **patellar tendinitis**, here's what it usually means:

### 🔎 Common Signs It’s Patellar Tendinitis
- Pain just **below the kneecap**
- Pain worsens with **activity**, especially jumping or squatting
- **Tenderness to touch** over the tendon
- **Stiffness or burning pain** after resting or first thing in the morning

### 🛠️ What to Do
- **Rest or modify activity**: Stop or reduce jumping, running, or kneeling.
- **Ice the area**: 15–20 minutes a few times per day to reduce inflammation.
- **Stretch & strengthen**: Focus on your **quads, hamstrings**, and **calves**. Eccentric quad exercises (like slow decline squats) are especially helpful.
- **Patellar strap or taping**: Can relieve pressure during activity.
- **NSAIDs** (like ibuprofen): Temporarily reduce inflammation and pain.
- **Physical therapy**: Highly recommended for guided recovery.

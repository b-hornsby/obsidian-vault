### ASSISTANT
You’re not tripping — you’re actually describing tater tots pretty accurately, and it’s kinda genius how they’re made.

**Here’s how a tater tot is made, step-by-step:**

1. **Raw potatoes are peeled and parboiled** (partially cooked) just enough to soften them slightly without turning them to mush.
2. **They’re grated or shredded**, which gives that signature “chopped-up potato” texture on the inside. It's like a hash brown vibe.
3. The shredded potato is **seasoned and mixed** with a bit of flour or cornstarch and sometimes dehydrated potato or a binder to help them stick together.
4. The mixture is then **formed into little cylindrical shapes** using a mold or an extruder — think of it like squeezing toothpaste but for potatoes.
5. These little cylinders are **blanched or pre-fried** briefly to help them hold their shape and start the crisping process.
6. Finally, they’re **frozen** (if they’re store-bought), and when you cook them — usually by deep frying, baking, or air frying — that’s when the **outside gets crispy**, thanks to the starches and the oil, while the inside stays tender and kind of fluffy/chunky.

That crispy shell? It’s mostly a combo of starch, oil, and the fact that the outer bits get more direct heat. The insides don’t feel mashed because they’re not — they’re still mostly shredded.

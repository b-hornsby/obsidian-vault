If you **haven’t left the company**, then **they cannot force a distribution** of your 401(k) — no matter how small the balance is.  

### Here’s why:
- Forced distributions are only allowed **after separation from employment** (you quit, got fired, laid off, etc.).
- While you're **still actively employed**, your 401(k) must stay **in your control**. Your employer **can’t touch it, move it, or cash it out** without your consent.

### If they did:
That would likely be a **violation of ERISA** (the federal law that governs retirement plans) and could have legal consequences. You’d have grounds to **file a complaint with the Department of Labor** or seek help from an attorney specializing in employee benefits.

### ASSISTANT
A **forced plan distribution** (also called a **mandatory distribution**) in a 401(k) refers to when your employer **automatically cashes out or rolls over your 401(k)** account without your active approval — usually **after you leave the company** and your account balance is below a certain threshold.

### Here's how it works:

- **If your 401(k) balance is under $1,000**:  
  Your employer can **cash out** the account and send you a check — **without your consent**. If you don’t roll it over within 60 days, it's considered a **taxable distribution** and possibly subject to a **10% early withdrawal penalty**.

- **If your balance is between $1,000 and $5,000**:  
  Your employer can’t just send you a check, but they **can roll it over** into an **IRA in your name** without asking you, if you don’t respond to their notices.

- **If your balance is over $5,000**:  
  The employer **can’t force a distribution** without your approval — they must leave the funds in the plan until you decide what to do.

### So yes, your employer **can** do a forced distribution **without your active approval**, but only if:
- You’ve left the company, **and**
- Your balance is **under $5,000** (with different rules based on the amount).

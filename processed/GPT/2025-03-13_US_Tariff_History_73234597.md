In that sense, what Trump is doing isn’t unprecedented. However, the controversy comes from a few key factors:  

1. **Scale and Aggressiveness** – While tariffs have always been part of trade policy, Trump’s approach is more aggressive, targeting key allies (like Canada and the EU) and major trade partners (like China) at an unprecedented scale. His trade war with China, for example, resulted in hundreds of billions of dollars in tariffs on both sides.  

2. **Retaliation and Economic Impact** – Countries hit with U.S. tariffs have responded with their own tariffs on American goods, which hurts U.S. businesses, especially in industries like agriculture and manufacturing. Many American farmers and manufacturers have suffered losses because they lost access to key export markets.  

3. **Consumer Prices** – Tariffs act as a tax on imports, meaning companies pass those costs on to consumers. Higher prices on goods like steel, electronics, and even everyday products (like washing machines) have made tariffs unpopular with many Americans.  

4. **Trade Relationships and Alliances** – Previous tariffs were often part of broader trade agreements, but Trump’s strategy has been more unilateral, sometimes straining relationships with allies. Some argue this approach weakens U.S. influence globally.  

5. **Short-Term vs. Long-Term Thinking** – Tariffs can protect certain industries in the short term, but in the long run, they can lead to inefficiencies and hurt economic growth. Many economists argue that free trade is ultimately better for economic prosperity.  

So while tariffs themselves aren’t new, Trump’s style of using them—at scale, unpredictably, and sometimes against allies—is what makes it controversial. His supporters argue that this “America First” approach forces other countries to play fair, while critics say it creates unnecessary economic harm and global instability.

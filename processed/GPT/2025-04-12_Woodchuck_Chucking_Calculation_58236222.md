Great question—yeah, that **700 pounds** figure is based on how much dirt a woodchuck typically moves **to dig a burrow**, not in a single day.

Richard Thomas estimated that a woodchuck moves about **35 cubic feet of dirt** when making its burrow. He then converted that dirt volume into an equivalent weight of wood—just for fun—coming up with **approximately 700 pounds of wood** total.

So to be clear:

- **Not per day**, but **per burrow**.
- And even that is just an estimate based on dirt-to-wood conversion—no woodchucks were seen actually tossing logs.

Alright, let’s do some quick squirrel–I mean, **woodchuck math**:

### Step 1: How much dirt does a woodchuck move total?
- About **35 cubic feet** per burrow.
- Let’s say it takes **about 2 weeks** to dig one burrow (a generous estimate based on wildlife studies).

### Step 2: Dirt per day?
- 35 cubic feet ÷ 14 days = **2.5 cubic feet of dirt per day**.

### Step 3: Convert dirt to wood (thanks to Richard Thomas):
- 1 cubic foot of dirt ≈ the chucking effort of 20 pounds of wood.
- So, 2.5 cubic feet/day × 20 pounds = **50 pounds of wood per day**.

Of course, woodchucks don’t actually chuck wood—they’re more into dirt-chucking. But it turns out:

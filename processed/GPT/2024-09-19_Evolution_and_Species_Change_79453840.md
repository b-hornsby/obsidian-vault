# Evolution and Species Change

### ASSISTANT
The common ancestor of both humans and modern monkeys was a primate species that lived about 25 to 30 million years ago. This ancestor was neither human nor monkey as we know them today but a more generalized primate. From this common ancestor, two main evolutionary branches emerged:

1. **Old World monkeys and apes (including humans):** These evolved in Africa and Asia. Humans, chimpanzees, gorillas, and orangutans belong to this group, along with baboons and macaques.
2. **New World monkeys:** These evolved in the Americas and include species like spider monkeys, capuchins, and tamarins.

Humans share a more recent common ancestor with chimpanzees and bonobos, which lived around 6 to 7 million years ago. From that point, humans and our ancestors continued evolving along a separate path.

### USER
Is evolution real if monkeys aren’t turning into humans nowadays in 2024?

### ASSISTANT
Yes, evolution is real, but it doesn’t mean that monkeys should be turning into humans today. Evolution is a slow process that happens over millions of years. Humans and modern monkeys evolved from a common ancestor, not from one another. That common ancestor lived millions of years ago, and since then, different species have evolved along their own paths.

Monkeys and humans are on separate branches of the evolutionary tree. Evolution isn't about one species "turning into" another, but rather how species adapt and change over long periods of time based on their environment, genetic mutations, and natural selection.

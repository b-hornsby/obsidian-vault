### Quick Background on Past Issues
In 2024–early 2025, Axiom faced significant challenges:
- **Cash crunch and operational strain**: Layoffs (around 100 people), voluntary pay cuts, supplier payment delays, and difficulties raising follow-on capital after earlier big rounds.
- **Delays**: The first station module (originally eyed for earlier attachment to the ISS) slipped to late 2026/2027. They also adjusted plans to a leaner design (e.g., focusing on a Payload Power Thermal Module or similar, with a two-module free-flying configuration targeted around 2028).
- **Valuation pressure**: A 2025 funding round was a "down round" at roughly $2 billion pre-money (down from a prior ~$2.6 billion peak).
- **Leadership turnover**: Multiple CEO changes in a short period.
- **Mission hiccups**: Ax-4 (their fourth private astronaut mission in 2025) saw delays from Falcon 9 booster issues, weather, and an unrelated ISS leak in the Russian Zvezda module—but it ultimately flew successfully.

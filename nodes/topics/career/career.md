# Career

*Pre-employment career hub, kept as reference now that the workforce transition is complete.*

---

## Where I'm coming from

I've spent 8 years in warehouse and logistics. Amazon Fulfillment Center. Shoe Dept Encore. I'm good at it — the operational discipline, the reliability, the ability to show up and grind every day. But it's not where I want to stay.

I don't have a CS degree. I don't have formal IT training. What I have is 3,400+ conversations with AI tools, a homelab that actually works, a custom PC I built with my own hands, and the stubborn belief that I can make this transition if I just keep pushing.

---

## What I'm targeting

**IT Support Specialist. Help Desk Technician. Technical Support Engineer. IT Operations.**

These are the roles where my homelab experience, my troubleshooting skills, and my self-taught technical knowledge actually matter. I'm not trying to be a software engineer — I'm trying to be the person who keeps systems running, who helps people when things break, who understands infrastructure from the ground up.

My LinkedIn headline says: *"IT Operations | Linux (Arch/CachyOS) · Win · Mac | AI Automation | Web3 Ops."* I lead with where I'm going, not where I've been.

---

## The journey so far

### Resume writing (dozens of iterations)
I've rewritten my resume more times than I can count. I've had AI tools help me frame warehouse experience in systems language — "managed high-volume inventory systems" instead of "moved boxes." I've made it ATS-friendly and personable at the same time.

I asked an AI to be my "resume reviewing professional charging $500" and make my accomplishments sound as personable as possible. I wanted bullet points that sounded like a real person wrote them, not a corporate drone.

### LinkedIn optimization
I built a custom PC — my first ever hands-on build. It took hours and I had to be extremely careful. I posted it on LinkedIn, but I wasn't satisfied with the description. I wanted it to stand out, to explain the passion behind the transition. So I kept iterating.

I've worked on narrowing down my professional niche. I've asked: *"Based on everything you've told me, here's how yours would look"* — and then refined the answers until they felt right.

### Certification path
I'm studying for CompTIA A+. I've asked for simplified explanations of Network+ acronyms because *"for me there's just a lot of acronyms and way too many numbers to remember."* I've worked through practice tests, analyzing each question I got wrong.

I'm also looking at SecAI certs and AI certs — the ones that will matter as the field evolves.

### The brutally honest review
I asked an AI to take on the role of my "brutally honest, high-level advisor." I told it: *"Speak to me like I'm a founder, creator, or leader with massive potential but who also has blind spots, weaknesses, or delusions that need to be cut through immediately. I don't want comfort."*

That's how I learn best. Direct, uncomfortable truth.

---

## What I bring to the table

**Operational discipline.** 8 years of showing up, meeting deadlines, and keeping operations running. In a warehouse, if you don't do your job, the whole line stops. That translates directly to IT support.

**Troubleshooting methodology.** I've debugged CUDA driver issues, WSL2 bootloader problems, audio routing nightmares, and dual-boot configurations. I work through problems methodically — isolate the issue, research the cause, test a fix, document the result.

**Self-taught technical depth.** I run CachyOS as my daily driver. I've set up Docker containers, configured Syncthing across devices, built a local LLM inference pipeline, and created multi-agent AI workflows. I didn't learn this in a classroom — I learned it by breaking things and fixing them.

**The homelab.** 793 conversations worth of infrastructure work. This is my portfolio. It's not a certification, but it's proof that I can build and maintain complex systems.

---

## What's blocking me

**No professional IT experience.** The classic catch-22: I need experience to get a job, but I need a job to get experience. The homelab and certifications are how I'm trying to break through.

**The warehouse gap.** My resume says 8 years of logistics. Hiring managers see "warehouse worker" and make assumptions. I need to get past the resume screen before they see what I can actually do.

**Imposter syndrome.** I asked an AI to cut through my "blind spots, weaknesses, and delusions." That's not something you ask for unless you know there's a gap between how you see yourself and how the world sees you.

**Certification progress.** I'm studying, but I haven't passed the A+ yet. Every month without a cert is a month where my resume doesn't check that box.

---

## The business idea

I've been thinking about this for a while. I want to build something at the intersection of AI and practical business solutions. I've brainstormed names like "Quick Fix Solutions" (even though I don't like how "car shop" it sounds). I've thought about tying together a Discord community, an LLC, a streaming presence, and a service booking platform.

I've asked: *"He helps me run AI models locally using open-source tools."* That's the niche. Practical AI solutions that people can actually use on their own machines. Not big tech dependency — local, open-source, self-sufficient.

But I haven't shipped it. It's still in the planning phase. And I know that's the pattern — I plan instead of shipping. This is the thing I'm actively fighting.

---

## What I need to do next

1. **Pass the CompTIA A+.** Stop studying, start scheduling. Pick a date and commit.
2. **Build the portfolio page.** A single page that showcases my homelab projects with screenshots and explanations.
3. **Apply to 10 jobs this week.** Not 10 applications to the same company. 10 different companies, tailored applications.
4. **Get the brutally honest review.** Find someone in IT — not an AI, a real person — and ask them to tear apart my resume and tell me what's missing.
5. **Ship the business idea.** Even if it's small. Even if it's just a landing page and a Discord server. Ship something.

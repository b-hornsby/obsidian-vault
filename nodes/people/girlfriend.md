# Girlfriend

*Building a life together in Akron, one crisis at a time. She's the reason I'm building something better.*

I live with my girlfriend and her dad. We're figuring out life together — car crises, credit scores, resume optimization, legal paperwork, and everything in between. It's not glamorous. It's real.

---

## Who she is

My girlfriend works at Shoe Dept Encore. She's smart, capable, and dealing with the same financial headwinds I am — a credit score around 520, a car she can't afford, and the kind of debt trap that feels impossible to escape.

She's not just someone I help with resumes and financial decisions. She's my partner in this. We're in the same boat, rowing in the same direction. When her car was facing repossession, it wasn't "her problem" — it was "our problem." When I'm applying for jobs, it's not just about me. It's about both of us building something better.

---

## The car crisis

The biggest storyline. She was 3 months into hardship extensions on a financed Honda, credit score at 520, facing repossession. $385/month she couldn't afford. The same trap I was in.

We researched everything. "Special finance" dealerships. Subprime lenders. Strategies to keep her on the road. The advice we got — from my dad, from AI tools, from forums — was consistent: do a lateral shift. Sell the Honda, extract the $1,100 in equity, buy a cheaper cash car. Get out of the debt trap.

It's the same advice I got about my own car. We did it together. That's what partnership looks like when money is tight — you solve the same problem twice, for two different people, with the same blunt solution.

---

## The resume work

I helped her rewrite her resume for a keyholder position at Shoe Dept Encore. ATS-friendly language. Professional framing. The same techniques I've used on my own resume dozens of times.

But here's the thing — I wasn't just helping her. I was practicing. Every resume I write for someone else makes me better at writing my own. Every bullet point I reframe for her teaches me something about framing my own experience.

She was also open-ended about her career direction, which meant the resume had to be flexible. We talked about keeping options broad while still sounding focused. That's a harder writing challenge than it sounds.

---

## The household paperwork

Living together means navigating bureaucracy as a unit. Ohio Form 20 asked us to define our relationship for legal purposes. "Girlfriend" not "spouse." Her dad's income affecting my eligibility for fee waivers. The kind of bureaucratic puzzle that only exists when your family situation doesn't fit neatly into government categories.

We spent multiple conversations on this — how to list the household, what titles to use, how to represent a living arrangement that doesn't have a checkbox. It's the unglamorous side of multigenerational living: the paperwork.

---

## Explaining the AI stuff

I tried to explain my AI project to her once — the agent, the vault, the second brain. I was trying to put it in simple, non-technical terms. That's harder than it sounds when you're building something that doesn't have an analog in everyday life.

How do you explain that you're building a digital version of yourself? That you're training an AI to think like you, know what you know, and help you make better decisions? That the 3,400 conversations you've had with AI tools are being woven into a knowledge graph that acts as a second brain?

I'm still working on that explanation. But the fact that I'm trying — that I want her to understand what I'm building and why — matters.

---

## What she means to me

She's the reason I'm doing this. Not in a "I need to provide for her" way — in a "we're building this together" way. Every certification I study for, every job application I send, every late-night debugging session — it's not just for me. It's for us.

She's also the person who keeps me grounded. When I'm spiraling about which AI tool is best, she's the one living in the real world, dealing with real problems, reminding me that the point of all this technology is to make life better — not to optimize for optimization's sake.
